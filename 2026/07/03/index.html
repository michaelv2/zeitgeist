Daily Memo (03-Jul-2026)

Key Themes

  • The labor cool-down is real, not the soft-landing the Dow record implies — and it, not the jobs headline, is what the front end should trade. June payrolls added just +57k (158,984k vs 158,927k) yet the unemployment rate "fell" to 4.2% — a drop driven by labor-force exit, not hiring, with continuing claims remaining elevated near 1,814k, roughly flat versus the ~1,821k tracked last week — historically high but not a new 2026 peak. The tape read this dovishly (Dow record, "cooling data buys the Fed time"). Direction is right — front-end rallies, cuts get pulled forward — but the reason is labor weakening, not Goldilocks. Confirm/refute: 2Y breaking below effective funds (3.63) flips the tape decisively to cuts; it sat at 4.17 pre-print (Jul 1) and should have fallen Jul 2.
  • The disinflation the market is banking on for July is a bet on oil, not a done deal. Headline CPI is re-accelerating — +0.47% MoM in May, a Feb–May run-rate near 6% annualized — even as core stays benign (+0.21%). The gap is goods/energy, likely tariff pass-through. The July relief thesis rests entirely on the WTI collapse ($70, down from ~$92 early June) offsetting that pass-through. If it doesn't, July 15 CPI disappoints a market already leaning dovish.
  • Rotation, not risk-off: Dow record while Nasdaq/semis fall. Cut hopes + soft data are rewarding cyclicals and rate-sensitives over the high-multiple AI cohort into late-July megacap earnings. This is a leadership inflection, not a top — but it argues underweight QQQ/semis, not sell equities. Quiet tail the tape is ignoring: private-credit stress ($14B gated, public credit funds unprofitable) — no transmission yet with financial conditions loose (CFCI -0.50), but the channel to watch.

Macro

Rates & Yields

  • Front-end should rally on the soft print; keep the bull-steepener bias. 10Y–2Y widened to +0.35 (Jul 2), 10Y–3M to +0.67 — steepening as cuts get pulled forward. 2Y at 4.17 (Jul 1, pre-jobs) still ~54bp above funds; the print should compress that.
  • Long-end supply risk still caps duration adds. Deficit-driven Q3 coupon supply and divided-government gridlock keep the 10Y (4.48) exposed. Add duration selectively at the front, not the long end.

Inflation

  • Core benign, headline hot — trust neither at face value. Core CPI +0.21%, core PCE +0.32% (~3.9% annualized, sticky); headline +0.47% MoM. Oil's collapse hasn't hit the CPI data yet (May print predates it), so July should mechanically ease — unless tariff goods pass-through (the source of the headline heat) offsets it. TIPS keep their hedge value on this headline re-accel.

Labor

  • Weaker than the 4.2% headline. Payroll growth has stalled (+57k), continuing claims remain elevated near 1,814k (roughly flat versus ~1,821k last week — historically high but not a new 2026 peak), and the unemployment drop looks like participation exit. JOLTS openings (7,594k, May) are the one firm-looking series — treat as lagging. Net: labor is loosening, which is the dovish input.

Consumer & Housing

  • The "healthy consumer" read from nominal retail is misleading — real spending is soft and choppy but still positive. May retail +0.88% nominal = ~+0.4% real, but April was slightly negative real. Layered on a series-low Michigan (44.8, from 49.8 in April), weak sentiment keeps an income squeeze a live risk, not a confirmed reality.
  • Interest-sensitive segments cracking. Housing starts collapsed to 1,177k (from 1,392k Apr, 1,522k Mar) — homebuilders/discretionary underweight validated.
