Key Themes

  • Oil is slipping into live Middle East kinetics — a growing disinflation engine on the tape. WTI has slipped to ~$79 (FRED $78.94, 22-Jun), off its highs but still well above $70, even as Iran struck Bahrain, Kuwait and a cargo vessel near Oman. The market is pricing the Hormuz tail as effectively dead and supply as ample. The catch: it's headline, not core (core PCE still +0.32% MoM). Tradeable read: a cheap Hormuz snapback hedge is now near-free optionality against a crowded short-oil book, while the disinflation read only rescues duration if June core CPI confirms.

Michigan sentiment cratered to 44.8 (May) — the low of the series, down from 56.6 in Feb — yet real retail held positive (+0.88% nominal May, ~+0.41% real on +0.47% CPI). The gap is real and unsustainable, but with real retail still positive the resolution is genuinely open: confidence could rebound toward spending, or spending could roll over toward sentiment. Stay cautious/neutral on consumer discretionary given the confidence collapse, with continuing claims grinding higher (1,821k, 13-Jun, up from ~1,758k in late April) and housing starts falling sharply to a multi-month low (1,177k May vs 1,392k Apr) adding downside risk. June retail (Jul 16) is the adjudicator.
  • The AI-capex bubble narrative just got institutional cover. BIS warned the AI investment boom is a financial-stability risk ("Liz Truss-style bonds crisis") the same week Samsung/SK Hynix announced a $600bn capacity build. The memory-cost squeeze is simultaneously an inflation vector and a mega-cap margin vector — two bad reads for QQQ. Stay underweight into late-July capex/margin guidance.
