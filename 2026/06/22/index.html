Key Themes

  • The 2Y is pricing Warsh hikes that the inflation data no longer justifies — that's the duration setup. The 2Y sits at 4.20%, ~57bp above effective funds (3.63%, May), so the curve is discounting net tightening into Warsh's first FOMC (Jun 24–25). But core is benign (core CPI cooled to +0.21% MoM in May after a firmer +0.38% April; core PCE +0.24% April) and the hot headline (+0.47% MoM May) is purely the war-energy spike now mechanically reversing as WTI collapses to $84.65 (−15% in two weeks). Warsh is fighting a fading enemy. Confirm: July 14 core CPI <0.25% + 10Y–2Y compressing further (already 0.27 from 0.40). Refute: core CPI ≥0.30% MoM. The near-term risk is a hawkish FOMC surprise pushing 2Y higher first — so add duration into strength, don't chase.
  • Housing is showing a tentative rate-sensitive signal, and the curve is consistent with a growth scare, not a re-acceleration. May starts fell to 1,177k (a 10-month low, from 1,392k in April); 10Y–2Y flattened to 0.27 and 10Y–3M to 0.63. The flattening-into-hikes pattern is consistent with the policy-error read — payrolls and CPI will carry the verdict.
  • China's rare-earth retaliation is a two-sided defense trade, not just a headline. Beijing's export curbs on US defense firms (answering the Pentagon blacklist) are a genuine input squeeze on the primes even as the order book booms — bullish ex-China rare-earth supply, a margin overhang for the manufacturers.
