Daily Memo (26-Oct-2026)

Bottom line: Credit is selling off while equity futures bounce. High-yield spreads are 10bp from my de-risk trigger. The Fed will probably hike into a consumer whose spending, after inflation, has stalled. Stay defensive on credit and equity beta. Keep the half-size 2Y long, but Oct 29 core PCE decides its size. Keep energy, tilted to producers.

Key Themes

  • High-yield spreads widened 72bp in a month to 3.40%, a move larger than 96% of monthly moves and at the 85th percentile of the last 3 years, while futures rally. The AI-builder unwind is one likely driver alongside oil and Fed hikes. Meanwhile futures are rallying on "AI storm abates" and a dip in crude.
    • Musk said 200+ neoclouds (rented-GPU cloud providers) face "guppy-level death," and SpaceX closed $40B of debt for its own GPUs. Both point to the same weak link: debt-funded AI builders that can't access large-scale financing, while compute demand moves to buyers that can.
    • Tell: this week's hyperscaler earnings plus high-yield spreads at 3.5%.
  • The Fed is about to hike into stalling real spending: September retail sales were roughly flat after inflation (+0.1%) after a +0.5% real Q3, and the 2Y at 4.65% sits ~50bp above the 4.125% midpoint of the favored year-end ceiling.
    • Prediction markets put the year-end Fed ceiling at 4.25% with 71% odds, and 96% odds of no cut in 2026. One more hike is consensus.
    • The 2Y at 4.65% sits about 50bp above the 4.125% midpoint of that 4.25% ceiling. That gap is some mix of residual 2027-hike risk and term premium, and either way it is room for the front end to rally if PCE is soft.
    • Demand doesn't support a sustained hiking path. September retail sales rose 0.77% nominal against +0.65% headline CPI, so roughly +0.1% after inflation. Michigan sentiment is 48.1, and housing starts are down 15% since June.
    • Inflation pricing treats the oil shock as front-loaded. The 5Y breakeven is 2.48% (79th percentile, +14bp in a month) while the 10Y is flat at 2.34%, which puts the 5-year rate five years out at about 2.2%. I read this as an oil tax on consumers, not an inflation spiral.
    • Risk to this view: Oct 29 core PCE.
  • The Gulf oil tail is fatter than the mediation-driven pullback implies (inference). The Houthis say they hit Saudi oil pumping stations. Pumping stations sit on the East-West pipeline, Saudi Arabia's main route around Hormuz. So the backup that would cap a Hormuz disruption is itself being targeted.
    • At the same time, Trump says a decision is nearing ("deal or finish it off"), and markets give 18.5% odds of a US invasion of Iran (up from 16.5%).
    • WTI at $108 is at the 98th percentile of the last 10 years. Hold XLE/XOP through mediation dips rather than trimming: the Houthi claim on Saudi pumping stations, if confirmed, threatens the Hormuz bypass. Add only on a confirmed outage, per the tell.

    • The 2Y fell 16bp on Oct 22 to 4.65%, still at the 91st percentile of the last 10 years.
    • The 10Y is 4.96%, with the 10Y–2Y spread at +0.31. The curve is nowhere near inverting.
    • Markets give only 7.9% odds of a 4.5%+ ceiling by year-end.
  • I'm holding the half-size 2Y long, not adding. The prior tell (the FOMC decision) hasn't resolved, and the calendar lists the FOMC for Nov 4–5. Oct 29 PCE comes first and sets the size.
  • No new evidence for the supply theme today. The long-end rally has moved against it. Keep avoiding ZROZ until early-November refunding gives a verdict.

Inflation

  • Core CPI has re-accelerated three months running: +0.22% in July, +0.29% in August, +0.335% in September, about 3.4% annualized. Headline CPI rose +0.65% in September.
  • Core PCE is softer but firming. The June–August pace annualizes to about 2.0%, but August alone was +0.25%. The Oct 29 September print decides whether the gap with CPI closes.
  • Breakevens confirm a front-loaded shock. The 5Y–10Y breakeven curve is now inverted (2.48% vs 2.34%), which is why I own short TIPS and not long TIPS.

Labor & Consumer

  • The labor market is supply-constrained, not slack.
    • Unemployment is 4.2% even though payrolls average only +68k a month since December (+29k in September).
    • Continuing claims fell to 1.73M from about 1.80M in August. Job openings are 7.08M.
    • Markets put deportations at 400–500k (58.5%), which keeps labor supply tight.
  • Q3 consumption was fine, but momentum stalled in September.
    • Over Q3, retail sales rose about 0.5% after inflation (nominal +1.65% vs CPI +1.12%).
    • September was roughly flat after inflation (+0.1%), and sentiment fell to 48.1 from 55.2 in July.
    • Industrial production rose 0.66% in September, so the weakness is in households, not factories.
    • Oct 29 Q3 GDP should print respectably and will understate the slowdown going into Q4.
