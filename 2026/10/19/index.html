Daily Memo (19-Oct-2026)

Bottom line

The US economy is running hotter than the slow hiring numbers suggest. Low payroll gains look like a shortage of workers, not a lack of demand, while output and spending are solid and core CPI has sped up three months running. I'm cutting the 2Y long to half size, keeping the inflation hedges (short TIPS and energy), and not reading today's oil dip as the all-clear. A reported Israeli election (date unconfirmed) plus Trump's rhetoric adds Iran tail risk — hold energy on the existing reasons: Russian refining hits and Houthi pressure on Saudi Arabia.

Key Themes

  • Slow hiring reflects too few workers, not weak demand, so the Fed has less room to stop than the "51k payrolls" story implies.
    • Payrolls are up only about 51k/month over three months. But unemployment fell from 4.4% in December to 4.2%, continuing claims fell from 1.78M to 1.71M, and job openings rose from 6.55M to 7.08M.
    • Betting markets put 2026 deportations at 400–500k (66.5%). My inference is that this is shrinking the labor force, so the number of jobs needed to hold unemployment steady is now well below the old norm.
    • The labor argument is weakened (unemployment is falling and openings are rising despite 51k payrolls). Cut to half size pending Nov 6, where unemployment of 4.4% or higher restores it. The other argument still stands conditionally: core PCE is softer than CPI on a 3-month basis (about 2.0% annualized May→Aug). But August was +0.25% and the YoY rate is 3.0%, so that softness depends on two weak summer months. Oct 29 PCE decides whether it holds.
    • Confirm with Nov 6 payrolls: unemployment at or below 4.2% on sub-75k payrolls backs the labor-shortage reading. Unemployment at 4.4% or higher rebuts it.
  • The oil market is pricing today's Russia sanctions waiver and missing the Iran tail risk tied to a reported Israeli election and escalating Houthi strikes.
    • Betting markets give Netanyahu only 28.5% to be the next PM. Trump says that if Netanyahu loses, the US will "finish the job" against Iran alone. A reported Israeli election (date unconfirmed) plus that rhetoric adds an Iran tail risk. The Houthis are exposing gaps in Saudi air defense, and Ukraine just hit oil facilities around St Petersburg.
    • My read: the sanctions waiver adds barrels on paper while drones remove real refining capacity. Downside for crude looks limited and the upside tail is growing. Breakevens at 2.35% are above their 10-year median (75th percentile) after a +13bp rise over 3 months, but flat this month despite the oil tail. That is some premium, arguably not enough.
    • Hold energy on the existing reasons: Russian refining hits and Houthi pressure on Saudi Arabia. Refuted if WTI holds below $90 after the vote with no strike on Iran.
  • An Anthropic IPO is now the base case (73.5% before 2027). That means a very large AI share sale lands just as AI stocks sell off and credit spreads widen.
    • Inference: it pulls money away from high-multiple AI names. It also puts a public price on the Anthropic stakes held by AMZN and GOOGL, which has not been forced on the market yet.

Macro

Rates & Fed

  • The front end prices about one more hike this year. The 2Y prices a 2027 hiking cycle on top of that.
    • Betting markets for the year-end upper bound: 4.25% at 67%, at least 4.5% at 12%.
    • The 2Y at 4.77% is about 89bp above effective fed funds (~3.88%). The 3M bill is about 4.28%.
    • The 2Y is down from 4.84% on Oct 5, so the long is in profit. Its stop (4.90%) was never hit.
  • The long-end rally since Oct 5 is about real yields, not inflation relief.
    • The 10Y fell 21bp (5.31% to 5.10%) while 10Y breakevens barely moved (2.36% to 2.35%).
    • The 10Y–2Y curve flattened 16bp in seven sessions to 0.35%. It is still positive and nowhere near inverting.
    • The long-end rally is real yields, not breakevens. The cause is unclear (flight to safety or a shrinking term premium), and the move cuts against the supply-rout theme, which is weakening rather than intact. Avoid ZROZ on the fiscal view, but nothing new arrived today to confirm it. A Democratic sweep (62.5%) means gridlock and no deficit cuts.

Inflation

  • Headline inflation is running about 4.6% annualized over three months and core CPI about 3.4% (Sept: headline +0.65% MoM, core +0.335%).
  • Core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, is softer on a 3-month basis (about 2.0% annualized May→Aug). But August was +0.25% and the YoY rate is 3.0%, so that softness depends on two weak summer months. The gap between the two gauges gets tested by the Sept PCE release on Oct 29, which comes after the Fed decides.
  • Breakevens look somewhat low given the oil tail. The 10Y breakeven is 2.35%, above its 10-year median (75th percentile) after a +13bp rise over 3 months — so not cheap on history. But it has been flat this month despite headline inflation running near twice the Fed's 2% target and the job market short of workers. That is some premium, arguably not enough.

Labor & Growth

  • Demand is real, not just higher prices.
    • Q3 retail sales rose 1.65% nominal against CPI up 1.12%, about 0.5% real.
    • Industrial production rose 0.66% in September and about 3.9% annualized over the quarter.
    • Betting markets: 2026 GDP growth above 2.5% at 47%, recession 6.5%.
  • Sentiment is weak (Michigan 51.7) but spending is fine. Weak sentiment is not stopping people from spending.
  • Housing is the soft spot: starts were 1.275M in August, and Case-Shiller prices are flat year-to-date.

Credit & Conditions

  • Credit is diverging from stocks.
    • High-yield spreads are 3.23%, in the 74th percentile of the last 3 years. They widened 47bp in a month, a bigger move than 89% of monthly moves in that window.
    • Financial conditions are tightening but still loose (Chicago Fed index –0.499, up from –0.549).
    • Stocks are buying the dip anyway. The de-risk trigger (HY at 3.5%) has not been hit.
