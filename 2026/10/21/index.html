Key Themes

  • HY spreads widened sharply, and stocks haven't noticed.
    • High-yield spreads (the extra yield on junk bonds over Treasuries) are at 3.29%, up 61bp in a month. That move is larger than 94% of monthly moves in the last 3 years, and the level is in the 80th percentile.
    • The yield curve flattened 18bp in two weeks (10Y–2Y from 0.51 to 0.33), led by falling long-term yields.
    • The 2Y yield fell after a hot September core CPI (4.82% to 4.77%).
    • Meanwhile, prediction markets still center the S&P 500's December close at 7,000–8,000 and give only about 9% odds of a dip to 6,200.
    • HY spreads widened sharply (+61bp in a month, a large move for the last 3 years) from tight levels. Broad financial conditions (NFCI -0.50) are still at their 10-year median and looser than average, so credit has started to tighten at the edges but is not yet doing the Fed's work. The cause of the widening is unclear. With growth data firm, it doesn't look default-driven yet. Whether it persists depends on the Oct 28 guidance and the 3.5% / 3.0% spread triggers. Stocks are the mispriced leg, not short-term rates.
    • Confirm: high-yield spread above 3.5%. Refute: back below 3.0%.
  • Breakevens already sit above their 10-year median, but haven't kept pace with oil.
    • The 10Y breakeven (the inflation rate bond markets expect over 10 years) is 2.35%, at the 75th percentile of the last decade and above its 2.22% median. It moved only 2bp in a month, quieter than 87% of months over the past decade.
    • The 5Y breakeven is 2.39% (70th percentile), up 8bp in a month.
    • Over the same period WTI oil rose $21 in three months to about $102, the 96th percentile of the past decade.
    • Headline CPI is 3.7% year-on-year and 4.6% annualized over three months. M2 money supply is growing about 6.4% annualized since November.
    • Breakevens already sit above their 10-year median (10Y 2.35%, 75th percentile; 5Y 2.39%), but they haven't kept pace with oil at $102 and 4.6% annualized headline CPI. Short-dated TIPS are a bet that the tail is under-priced, not that the market expects a fade. Refute: breakeven below 2.25%.

Macro

Rates & Fed

  • Short-term rates already price too many hikes.
    • The 2Y at 4.77% is about 89bp above the effective fed funds rate (about 3.88% after September's hike; the September average was 3.75% vs 3.63% in August).
    • It is still about 64bp above where effective funds would sit (about 4.13%) if the 4.25% upper bound that markets give 67% for year-end arrives.
    • Prediction markets give about 80% odds of one more hike by year-end and about 10% of two. The 2Y is pricing hikes deep into 2027 as well.
  • The 3-month bill is about 4.29%, right at my 4.3% exit line. October's hike is fully priced, plus part of December.
  • The long-end rally is in real yields, not inflation expectations. The 10Y fell from 5.27% to 5.15% while breakevens held at 2.35%, putting the 10Y real yield at about 2.80%. The rally is in real yields, and its cause is unclear (flight to safety from credit, or a smaller term premium). The supply story is fading as the driver. The November Treasury refunding is its next test.

Inflation

  • Core CPI has re-accelerated three months running: +0.22%, +0.29%, +0.335% in July–September, about 3.4% annualized.
  • The CPI–PCE gap is still open. Core PCE (the Fed's preferred gauge) rose +0.13%, +0.13%, +0.25% in June–August, about 2.0% annualized over three months.
  • The October 29 GDP report effectively reveals September core PCE early, the day after the Fed decides. Taking July and August as printed:
    • Q3 core PCE at or above about 2.4% annualized implies September at or above about 0.33%. The gap closes.
    • Q3 at or below about 2.2% implies September at or below about 0.20%. The summer softness holds.
    • Revisions to July/August could blur this.

Labor

  • The labor market is tight, not slack.
    • Payroll growth averages only about 51k a month over three months.
    • But unemployment is 4.2% (down from 4.4% in December), continuing jobless claims of 1.71M are the lowest in the recent data, and job openings are 7.08M.
    • Hiring and firing are both low and labor supply is constrained (markets give 67% that Trump deports 400–500k people).
    • September's uptick from 4.1% to 4.2% is noise until the November 6 jobs report.

Growth, Consumer, Housing

  • Growth isn't the problem. Industrial production is up 2.4% since December. September retail sales rose +0.77% nominal, but after the +0.65% headline CPI that is only about +0.1% real; Q3 real retail is about +0.5% quarter-on-quarter. Recession odds are 7.5%.
  • Housing is softening. Starts are at 1,221k, the 16th percentile of the last decade and about 11% below December. On 3-month averages they are about 4% below Q2. Case-Shiller home prices are up 1.3% over nine months against CPI up 3.2%, so real home prices are falling.

  • Middle East: Netanyahu is 41.5% to be out by year-end and Eisenkot 51.5% to be the next PM. A US invasion of Iran before 2027 is at 15.5%. The oil upside tail is intact.

  • Long 2Y Treasuries, half size, because the 2Y is pricing hikes well beyond the roughly one more that prediction markets expect, and it fell even on a hot CPI.
  • Overweight short TIPS (STIP/VTIP) over T-bills, because the 5Y breakeven (2.39%) still sits well below 3.7% headline CPI and 4.6% 3-month annualized, with the oil tail live.
  • Keep HYG puts and stay underweight high-beta equities, because credit has widened 61bp while S&P prediction markets show little fear. De-risk further at a 3.5% spread.
  • AI barbell: overweight MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL and chip-equipment makers (ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC); underweight borrowing-funded builders (CRWV, IREN, ORCL), because widening credit hits the borrowers and the Anthropic IPO (78%) adds share supply to high-multiple AI.
