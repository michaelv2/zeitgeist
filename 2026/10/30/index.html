Daily Memo (30-Oct-2026)

Bottom line: Mega-cap AI stocks are selling off just as the inflation data took away any chance of Fed help. Alphabet lost about $500B in its worst day ever, and Intel fell 21%. Meanwhile September core PCE (0.32%) roughly matched core CPI for the month and narrowed the trend gap (about 2.9% vs 3.4% annualized over three months). That's enough to exit the 2Y long. I'm exiting the 2Y long, keeping front-end TIPS, staying underweight the companies spending heavily on AI, adding to the HY credit hedge while it's still cheap, and holding energy into the post-election window.

Key Themes

  • No Fed backstop under this tech selloff. In past drawdowns, investors could count on rate cuts. This time inflation rules that out.
    • Prediction markets give 97% odds of no cut in 2026. A 4.25% year-end ceiling is the 71% favorite.
    • Core PCE rose 0.32% in September, and core CPI has re-accelerated for three months (0.22%, 0.29%, 0.335%).
    • The 10Y is at 5.05%, and its inflation-adjusted yield is about 2.7%, a heavy discount rate for long-duration growth stocks.
    • So I'd expect the de-rating of high-multiple tech to grind on rather than snap back.
    • What would refute it: the 2Y falling below about 4.50% while December-hike odds also fall. That would mean the bond market is starting to price a rescue.
  • Investors now punish AI spending instead of rewarding it. That pressure is how 2027 budgets get cut, so suppliers are hit last, not spared. (This is my inference.)
    • Alphabet, Amazon and Intel results "deepened AI spending woes."
    • Odds that NVDA is the world's largest company at year-end fell to 52% from 68.5% in the last memo. Apple is at 44.6%.
    • Suppliers like memory makers are still being paid in cash on budgets already committed (SK Hynix profit up 7x). The risk is 2027 spending guidance being cut.
    • Credit hasn't reacted. HY spreads were 3.14% and the VIX 16.9 on Oct 28, both before the crash. The AI builders that borrow to fund data centers are where stock-market pain turns into credit pain.
  • Both oil supply risks reopened at once. Trump downplayed Iran peace talks while the US has warned allies of strikes after Nov 3. Separately, Ukraine resumed hitting Russian refineries, defying Trump. That oil pressure lands on top of re-accelerating core inflation.

Macro

Rates & Inflation

  • The monthly gap between core PCE and core CPI has closed, and the trend gap has narrowed (prior theme confirmed; the 2Y view has turned).
    • September core PCE rose 0.32%, after 0.26% in August and 0.14% in July. That's about 2.9% annualized over three months.
    • Core CPI re-accelerated for three months (0.22%, 0.29%, 0.335%), running about 3.4% annualized; headline CPI rose 0.65%.
    • The last memo's exit trigger was 0.33%. This print is 1bp short, but the condition behind it (the gap narrowing) is met, so I'm exiting rather than hiding behind rounding.
  • The 2Y has no edge left. At 4.67% it sits about 55bp above the midpoint of the favored 4.25% ceiling, so it already prices hikes into 2027.
    • With inflation running at about 3%+ and the labor market not loosening, the market's hike pricing looks fair.
    • M2 is up 4.9% since December (about 6.5% annualized), which also leans hawkish.
  • The long end is back above 5%. The 10Y is at 5.05%, well short of the 5.3% level that would revive the fiscal-supply thesis, which stays shelved. The 10Y-2Y spread has re-steepened to +0.37 from +0.29.
  • Breakevens: short-term inflation expectations are rising while long-term ones are stuck.
    • The 5Y breakeven is 2.47%, up 13bp in a month and in the 78th percentile of the last 10 years. The 10Y breakeven is flat at 2.35%.
    • That leaves 5Y breakevens about 12bp above 10Y. The market still treats the oil shock as front-loaded, which keeps front-end TIPS the better tool than long TIPS.

Growth, Labor & Consumer

  • Activity is too firm for the Fed to come to the rescue.
    • September industrial production rose 0.66%, the best month of the run.
    • Continuing claims fell to 1.70M from 1.775M in August.
    • Unemployment is 4.2%, and job openings are 7.08M.
  • Hiring is weak, but it reads as a labor-supply shortage, not falling demand.
    • Payrolls have averaged about 51k a month over three months.
    • The deportation market's most likely bucket is 400–500k people (49%).
    • Low unemployment alongside weak payrolls points hawkish.
  • Consumers are squeezed but still spending.
    • Retail sales rose 0.77% in nominal terms, roughly 0.1% after inflation.
    • Michigan sentiment is 48.1.
    • Losing about $500B in Alphabet value alone is a new wealth-effect hit on top of that.
  • Housing is weakening. Starts at 1.221M have fallen three months in a row and sit at the 16th percentile of the last 10 years, with the 10Y near 5%. Home prices are only edging up (Case-Shiller 334.2).
  • Financial conditions are still loose but drifting tighter. The Chicago Fed index has risen nine straight weeks to -0.487, still below zero. The moves are small and the data predates the crash.
