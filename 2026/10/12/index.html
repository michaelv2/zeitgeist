Daily Memo (12-Oct-2026)

Key Themes

  • Inflation protection is cheap just as September headline CPI is set to come in hot. I think the market is underpricing it.
    • Regular gasoline averaged ~$4.36 in September vs ~$4.06 in August, up ~7% (GASREGW). That alone mechanically lifts the Oct 14 headline print.
    • The 10Y breakeven still slipped to 2.33% (T10YIE). Headline CPI has run ~3.7% annualized since November.
    • The 10Y at ~5.2% is mostly real yield (10Y TIPS ~2.9%, DFII10), not inflation fear.
    • My read: front-end TIPS over nominals as a tactical hedge into a mechanically hot Oct 14 headline print (September gasoline averaged ~$4.36 vs ~$4.06 in August). The carry edge fades if headline peaks in September, so reassess after the PCE print on Oct 30.
    • What would kill it: core CPI ≤0.2% with breakevens falling below 2.25%.
  • Financial conditions are tightening while stocks sit at records. Stocks are the outlier, not the signal.
    • The Chicago Fed index (NFCI) has risen six weeks in a row (−0.551 → −0.494). It is still loose, but moving the wrong way.
    • High-yield spreads widened from 2.93% to 3.15% in two weeks (BAMLH0A0HYM2).
    • The broad dollar is up 1.7% since Sep 21.
    • The effective fed funds rate stepped up to 3.88% in late September, which reads as a hike.
    • My inference: M2 growth of ~6% annualized has been cushioning equities. That cushion is not unlimited.
    • What would confirm it: high-yield spreads above 3.5% before the Oct 29 GDP print.
  • The diesel deal may signal a White House push to lean on fuel prices before the midterms. It is contested and may not hold. This is my inference.
    • Evidence: the Russian diesel purchase deal faces pushback from Republicans, Europe and Zelensky, and Trump's claimed Ukraine "energy ceasefire" is already reported as violated.
    • The refiner case loses its crack-spread tailwind because the hurricane trigger missed: Isaias hit Florida and Alabama, not the Texas/Louisiana refining core.
    • It also means September is likely the peak headline CPI print — gasoline is already rolling over at $4.35 on Oct 5, down from late-September highs.

Macro

Rates & Yields

  • The front end is still pricing more hikes, not cuts.
    • With effective funds at 3.88% after September's ~25bp step-up, the 2Y sits ~85–90bp above funds and 3M bills ~35bp above. That is consistent with roughly one more hike priced, partly bill supply. The 2Y is drifting lower (4.92%→4.75%), so the front end is leaning dovish at the margin.
    • The prior "hike pause" framing is too dovish.
  • The long end is driven by real yields and term premium, not inflation fear. I'd keep the steepener but not add to it.
    • 10Y at 5.22%, off the 5.31% high; 10Y TIPS ~2.9%; breakevens flat at ~2.35%.
    • 10Y–2Y has stalled at 0.44–0.51. Both ends rallied together last week, so the curve didn't move.
  • The FOMC date is unclear. The schedule in hand lists Nov 4–5; prior memos cited Oct 28. Confirm before positioning for it.

Inflation

  • Headline CPI is mechanically hot for September, so core is the real test.
    • August core CPI +0.29% MoM after +0.22% in July. Core PCE +0.25%.
    • The 3-month annualized core rate (~2.0%) is flattered by June's −0.02%. The 9-month rate is ~2.7%.
    • Since November, core PCE (~3.2% annualized) has run hotter than core CPI.
  • Base case: core comes in around 0.3%. That keeps the stagflation box intact. October gasoline is already rolling over ($4.35 on Oct 5), so headline should ease from here.

Labor & Consumer

  • Labor is slow but not cracking, so the Fed gets no cover to cut.
    • Payrolls have averaged ~68k/month since December; September was +29k.
    • Continuing claims fell to 1.72M from 1.80M in July. Unemployment is 4.2%.
    • My inference: weak hiring reflects tighter labor supply (immigration curbs), not layoffs.
  • The consumer-squeeze thesis is fading. August retail sales rose ~0.7% after inflation, while Michigan sentiment sits at 51.7. A second straight inflation-adjusted gain in September retail sales, after July's real decline, would close it.
  • Housing is weakening.
    • Starts fell to 1,275k from 1,522k in March.
    • Case-Shiller is up ~1.3% over nine months, so home prices are falling after inflation.

Credit & Liquidity

  • High-yield spreads near 3.1% are still tight, but the direction has turned. That makes credit protection cheap.
