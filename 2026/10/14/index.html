Daily Memo (14-Oct-2026)

Bottom line: The Fed is hiking with hiring near zero, and core inflation decides the path. Oil is an added upside risk to headline and expectations. The very front of the curve already partly prices an October 28 hike. The 2Y still prices more tightening than core inflation justifies, so I stay overweight the 2Y and underweight the long end. September PCE now lands after the FOMC (Oct 30, not Oct 29 as previously cited). That makes this morning's CPI and tomorrow's PPI the inputs that actually decide the Fed's move.

Key Themes

  • The front end has moved from "overpricing hikes" to "pricing the Fed it has." The edge now sits at 2Y, not 3M.
    • The 3M bill is at ~4.06%, up 25bp in a month (larger than ~88% of monthly moves in the last decade), ~18bp over effective funds of 3.88%, after the September hike. An October 28 hike is partly priced.
    • The 2Y at 4.80% sits 92bp over funds. My inference, ignoring term premium, is that this needs roughly four more hikes to lose on carry.
    • Core PCE does not justify that: 3-month core PCE is 2.05% annualized, flattered by soft June/July. August re-accelerated to +0.25%, and YoY (3.0%) and the since-November run-rate (~3.25%) are higher. The 2Y long is a bet that the summer softness holds, and today's core CPI tests it.
    • The catch: July–August core CPI annualizes to about 3.1%. Today's core print decides whether the 2Y long survives.
  • Oil is an added upside risk to headline inflation and expectations, and the Gulf escalation keeps climbing.
    • A Houthi strike hit a building at Saudi Aramco's headquarters. The sequence so far: Riyadh airport, then missiles toward Riyadh, now Aramco property.
    • WTI is at $96, up $26.6 in three months. That move is larger than 94% of three-month moves in the last decade, and the price level is in the 94th percentile.
    • The 10Y breakeven (2.33%) barely moved over the month (−3bp), so long-dated pricing treats oil as transitory. The Fed's September hike says it doesn't.
    • Inference: energy is the hedge that pays in exactly the scenario where the rate-sensitive book loses. Confirm: a hit on Saudi production or processing sites, with WTI holding above $100. Refute: WTI below $90.
  • ASML's third guidance raise shows physical AI capex is still accelerating at the tool layer. The funding and monetization side of the crack thesis is untested until hyperscaler earnings and the Oct 29 GDP investment line.
    • ASML raised guidance for the third time and says demand exceeds chip-tool capacity through 2028.
    • Spending on physical AI build-out is not slowing. The fragility is in who funds it, with a 10Y at 5.24%.
    • The equity market prices this as one trade. It should be split into scarce suppliers with pricing power (long) and borrowed-money builders (short).

Macro

Rates & Fed

  • Labor is not what's stopping the Fed.
    • Payrolls average about 51k over three months (September +29k).
    • But unemployment is 4.2% and continuing claims fell to 1.716M from 1.799M in late July.
    • Inference: this is a low-hiring, low-firing market with a low breakeven payroll pace. It isn't loose enough to force a pause, so inflation alone sets the path.
  • The curve is range-bound, not in a clear trend.
    • 2s10s has retraced to 42bp from a 51bp spike on Oct 7. It is still up ~9bp on the month and near its 10-year median, so the curve is range-bound. I'm dropping the stand-alone steepener for lack of momentum and expressing the view as long 2Y / avoid the long end.
  • The long end is real yield and term premium, not inflation fear.
    • 10Y at 5.24% minus a 2.33% breakeven leaves a ~2.9% real yield.
    • This is intact. The Nov 5 refunding is the next test.

Inflation

  • Headline will run hot. Core is the only thing that matters today.
    • August headline was +0.40% and core +0.29%.
    • Core PCE has run about 3.25% annualized since November. The soft summer is two months, not a regime.

Consumer & Housing

  • The consumer squeeze thesis is fading.
    • August retail sales were +1.13% nominal, about +0.7% real.
    • Retail is up 0.9% nominal over three months against +0.05% CPI.
    • Spending is beating sentiment (Michigan 51.7). The Oct 16 retail print settles it.
  • Housing is rolling over.
    • Starts were 1,275k in August versus 1,522k in March.
    • Case-Shiller is roughly flat year to date.
    • The homebuilder underweight holds on a 10Y above 5.2%.

Conditions

    • The Chicago Fed index (−0.494) is still looser than average but has drifted tighter from −0.551.
    • The broad dollar index is 121.6, up from 120.7 on Sep 28. A hawkish Fed keeps supporting the dollar.

Positioning Summary

  • Overweight 2Y Treasuries (SHY / 2Y note), because the 2Y at 4.80% sits 92bp over funds, so roughly four hikes would be needed to lose on carry (ignoring term premium). Part of that spread is term premium, not hike expectations. The bet is that the summer softness in core PCE holds; today's CPI tests it.
  • Underweight long duration (ZROZ), because the 10Y's ~2.9% real yield is a supply and term-premium story into the Nov 5 refunding, and gridlock offers no relief.
  • Hold front-end TIPS (VTIP) as carry, because oil +$26 over three months feeds headline CPI accrual that a flat 10Y breakeven ignores.
  • Hold upstream energy (XLE) as the portfolio hedge, because the escalation path pays off exactly when the rate-sensitive book loses.
  • Overweight ASML and chip equipment against underweight CRWV, IREN and ORCL, because physical scarcity means pricing power while borrowed-money builders absorb a 5%+ cost of capital.
  • Overweight defense (XAR, EUAD, LMT, RTX), because demand is coming from several theatres at once and Europe is funding its own.
  • Hold HYG puts as a hedge against the Fed hiking into ~50k-a-month payrolls. HY at 3.11% is mid-range for the last 3 years after an unusually large +46bp monthly widening that has stalled since early October, not reversed. Protection is fairly priced, not cheap.
  • Avoid: homebuilders, refiners, and chasing EWZ.

Key Tells

  • Today's core CPI: 0.25% or less supports adding to the 2Y. 0.35% or more, with the 2Y back above 4.9%, means cutting the front-end long.
  • 3M bill vs. funds after the Oct 28 FOMC: if the 3M bill stays more than 18bp above funds after a hike, December is priced too, and the hiking cycle is longer than my 2Y call assumes.
  • WTI: holding above $100 on a strike at Saudi production sites adds to energy and cuts duration. Below $90 means trimming energy.
  • HY spreads: above 3.5% before Oct 29 GDP means taking down beta. Below 3.0% kills the credit hedge.
