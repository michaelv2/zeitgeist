Daily Memo (16-Oct-2026)

Key Themes

  • The curve is pricing a Fed that wins, and so far the bond market agrees with it (the 10Y–3M spread fell from 1.09 to 0.82 in two weeks, a large move; the 10Y fell to 5.19% from a 5.31% high even after a hot CPI; the 10Y breakeven sits at 2.32%, down 6bp on the month, while WTI hit $101.91).
    • What's mispriced: inflation compensation. Headline CPI is running about 4.6% annualized over three months and producer prices rose 6.3% YoY, but breakevens treat oil as transitory.
    • What I'm betting: realized inflation stays above breakevens while the Fed delivers fewer hikes than the 2Y implies.
    • Confirm: breakevens rising with oil after the Oct 28 FOMC. Kill: oil back below $90.
  • Real spending slowed sharply in September (+0.1% after +0.9% in August), and hiring is near stall speed. The Fed is hiking into a slowing economy, and Oct 29 GDP/PCE is the test of whether it has stalled.
    • Sept retail sales rose about 0.1% after inflation (+0.77% before), down from about +0.9% in August.
    • Payrolls averaged +51k over three months, and unemployment rose to 4.2%.
    • Credit has noticed: high-yield spreads are 3.25%, up 54bp in a month, a move bigger than 91% of monthly moves in the last 3 years. Stocks near records are the outlier.
    • The Oct 29 GDP and PCE data land after the Oct 28 decision, so the Fed hikes before seeing them.

Macro

Inflation

  • Core inflation has re-accelerated three months running, so the soft summer is over. Core CPI rose +0.22% in July, +0.29% in August and +0.335% in September, about 3.4% annualized over three months.
    • That misses the ≤0.25% "cooling" bar I set and sits just under the ≥0.35% "stagflation" bar.
  • Headline inflation is the bigger problem (Sept CPI +0.65% MoM). Sept core PCE (Oct 29) is likely firm given the CPI and PPI (inference). It lands after the Fed decides.

Labor & Consumer

  • Hiring is near stall speed but layoffs aren't rising. Payrolls rose +29k in September; continuing claims are 1.71M, down from 1.78M in August; job openings are 7.08M. This is a low-hire, low-fire market, not a recession yet.
  • The resolution I wanted (a third solid real gain) didn't arrive, but there was no real-negative reversal either. The squeeze thesis is unresolved, not reasserting.
    • Spending growth after inflation fell to about +0.1% in September, and higher gas prices inflate the dollar figure, so the non-fuel picture is likely weaker (inference).
    • Last memo, I said a third straight solid real-spending gain would settle this. That didn't happen: July was negative and September was barely positive. Oct 29 real consumption is the decider.

Rates & Fed

  • An Oct 28 hike is fully priced, and December is partly priced. The 3-month bill is 4.28% against fed funds of 3.88%, a 40bp gap, and it rose 17bp in a month.
  • The 2-year at 4.82% is 94bp over fed funds, which is up to about four hikes if the term premium is zero, and fewer if the term premium is positive. That is still a demanding path into 51k jobs a month.
    • Neither stop-loss on the long 2Y position triggered (core wasn't ≥0.35% and the 2Y isn't >4.90%), so hold it.
  • The long end is trading growth, not supply, for now. The 10Y fell even as the 2Y rose, and the 10Y real yield is about 2.84% (5.19% nominal minus 2.35% breakeven).
    • The fiscal/term-premium rout theme is paused, not dead. With Congress likely gridlocked, deficits aren't getting fixed.

Credit & Equities

  • HY spreads are back at their Oct 1 high (3.25%), after a 54bp rise over the month, larger than 91% of monthly moves in the last 3 years. The level is 76th percentile of that short window and below my 3.5% de-risk trigger.
  • Financial conditions have tightened for 10 straight weeks but remain loose (Chicago Fed index −0.549 to −0.499; below zero means looser than average). Stocks "nudging higher" near records leaves the market's extra return over bonds thin.
