Daily Memo (23-Oct-2026)

Bottom line

Bonds have started hedging stocks again. Treasuries had their biggest drop in yields since the 2023 banking crisis while oil hit $114, which tells you the market fears the AI selloff more than inflation. The Fed will still raise rates on Oct 28. I keep the edge in the 2-year Treasury and in short-dated inflation-protected bonds (TIPS), not in long bonds. Energy and defense stay overweight, and I'm adding to defense.

Key Themes

  • The safe-haven bid is back, and the AI selloff is driving it, not the Fed. Yields fell hard even as oil hit a five-month high of $114 and the 5Y breakeven (the inflation rate priced into 5-year bonds) rose. The market is treating a hit to the AI trade as a hit to growth.
    • The move skipped the very front end. The 3M bill held at ~4.25%, derived from the 10Y–3M spread falling to 0.74. The 2Y fell ~13bp to ~4.7%, derived from a 10Y below 5% and a 10Y–2Y spread of 0.31. So the October hike is still priced; what got removed was 2027 hikes.
    • The long end led the rally, which again cuts against the supply thesis. I keep avoiding ZROZ only because 31bp of 10Y–2Y curve doesn't pay for refunding risk. The early-November refunding decides it.
    • Confirm or kill: Microsoft's late-October capex and margin guidance, plus the Oct 29 GDP equipment/software investment line. If capex is cut, the growth scare is real and the haven trade lasts. If it rises, the long-end rally gives back. Alphabet and Amazon earnings will add to the picture once their dates are confirmed.
  • The inflation curve has inverted: the market sees the oil shock as front-loaded. The 5Y breakeven jumped to 2.45% (76th percentile of the last 10 years) while the 10Y fell to 2.32%. The 5y5y forward fell 15bp to 2.19%, right at its 10-year median. The market sees the oil shock as front-loaded, with longer-run inflation expectations back at normal.
    • Short TIPS capture the oil pass-through. Long TIPS don't, because long-dated expectations are anchored rather than pricing additional demand pressure. The prior TIPS tell (5Y above ~2.45% with oil over $100) has tripped, but only at the front end.
  • The Fed is constrained by labor supply, not demand, so weak payroll numbers won't save bond bulls. Payrolls are averaging +51k a month over three months (+29k in September). Yet unemployment fell to 4.2% from 4.4% in December, and job openings are 7.08M. Weak payrolls with unemployment falling to 4.2% fit a supply-constrained market. Nov 6 (unemployment ≤4.2% on weak payrolls) is the confirmation. Prediction markets give 60.5% to 400–500k deportations.
    • The 2Y can still fall by removing hikes priced into 2027. It cannot price cuts: markets give 96% to no cut in 2026.

Macro

Rates & Yields

  • The 2Y still prices too many hikes. At ~4.7% it sits ~80bp above effective fed funds (~3.88%) and ~55bp above the midpoint if the 4.25% year-end ceiling arrives (68.5% odds). An upper bound of 4.5% or more by year-end is only 8.3%. The Oct 28 FOMC sizes the trade.
  • The 10Y rally came from real yields, not inflation expectations. The 10Y real yield fell from ~2.76% to ~2.67%, derived from the 10Y below 5% minus a 2.32% breakeven. Even so, the 10Y was at the 99th percentile of its 10-year range on Oct 21. Prediction markets give 17.85% to the 10Y hitting 5.5% before 2027 and ~3% to it falling below 3.9%. Both tails are thin, so this is a range trade, not a breakout.

Inflation

  • Core CPI is accelerating. It rose +0.22%, +0.29% and +0.335% in July, August and September, ~3.4% annualized. Headline CPI jumped +0.65% in September alone.
  • The "soft core PCE" story only covers the summer. The last three months annualize to ~2.0%, but December–August annualizes to ~3.2% (computed from the series). The Q3 GDP release (Oct 29) and September core PCE (Oct 30) decide it: September at ≥0.33% closes the gap with CPI, ≤0.20% keeps the summer softness.
  • October CPI (Nov 10) will carry another energy hit, because $114 oil is above the WTI range in the data so far.

Labor

  • The labor market is tight on supply (see Key Themes). Continuing jobless claims rose 25k to 1.728M, which is noise against a range of 1.70–1.80M.

Growth & Consumer

  • Real consumer spending is thinning. September retail sales rose +0.77% nominal but only ~+0.1% after inflation. Q3 real retail is still ~+2% annualized, held up by a strong August. Michigan sentiment is 51.7.
  • Industry is firm: industrial production rose +0.66% in September.
  • Housing is rolling over: starts fell to 1.221M from 1.439M in June.
  • Recession odds are 6.5%, so the tape is reading stagflation, not a slump.

Credit & Conditions

  • Credit is widening but hasn't hit my trigger. High-yield spreads were 3.27% on Oct 21, up 61bp in a month, a move larger than 94% of monthly moves in the last 3 years. That is still below my 3.5% de-risk trigger, and the Oct 22 tech rout isn't in the data yet.
  • Financial conditions are tightening slowly: the Chicago Fed index (NFCI) moved from -0.55 to -0.50 over nine weeks. That is still loose, but it has tightened every week.

  • AI: keep the barbell intact.

  • Consumer discretionary and airlines: underweight. Q3 real retail held ~+2% annualized, but September's real gain slowed to ~+0.1% and $114 oil points to a squeeze in Q4. Nov 16 retail sales is the confirmation.
  • Healthcare:
    • MRK is the cleaner way to own the melanoma vaccine news than Moderna, which sold off even on positive data.
    • The early flu season is a fourth-quarter medical-cost risk for managed care (UNH, HUM). That's an inference; treat it as a watch item.

Positioning Summary

  • Hold the 2Y long at half size into Oct 28. At ~4.7% it prices hikes well beyond the 4.25% ceiling markets see as base; the odds of 4.5% or more are only 8.3%.
  • Overweight short TIPS (STIP/VTIP) over T-bills, because the 5Y breakeven broke 2.45% with oil at $114 while bills pay nothing extra for the oil pass-through.
  • Don't extend into long TIPS (TIPZ/LTPZ). The 10Y breakeven (2.32%, 67th percentile) is flat on the month and long-dated expectations are anchored, so long TIPS don't capture the oil pass-through the way short TIPS do.
  • Avoid ZROZ; if you want a duration hedge, use 2–5Y (SHY/IEI), because a 31bp 10Y–2Y spread doesn't pay for refunding supply risk.
  • Overweight energy (XLE/XOP), with refiners underweight relative to producers, because a peace deal at 9.5% leaves the upside tail underpriced.
  • Add to defense (XAR/EUAD/RTX), because NATO–Russia clash odds rose 8 points and Europe has no satellite substitute.
  • Keep the AI barbell and add AAPL vs NVDA, because the rout and wider credit hit borrowed-money builders first, and AAPL's catalyst is a product cycle, not capex.
  • Keep HYG puts, because spreads moved 61bp in a month and the Oct 22 tech rout isn't in the data yet.
  • Underweight discretionary and airlines, because September's real retail gain slowed to ~+0.1% and $114 oil points to a squeeze in Q4.
