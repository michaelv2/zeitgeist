Daily Memo (13-Oct-2026)

Bottom line: The front end is pricing more Fed hikes than the Fed's preferred inflation gauge supports, and early strain is showing at the cyclical end of the AI trade (a memory selloff, high-yield spreads widening from tight levels) while stocks sit near records. My calls: own the 2Y over long bonds, buy cheap high-yield credit protection, cut AI build-out exposure, and keep the defense overweight. Tomorrow's CPI is the first test.

Key Themes

  • Core PCE has slowed to about 2% annualized over three months, but August re-accelerated to +0.25% and it is still 3.0% year over year. The cooling is two soft months, not a settled 2% run-rate.
    • Data: core PCE rose 2.0% annualized over the three months to August, after +0.13% in June and July and +0.25% in August. The effective funds rate stepped up to 3.88% in late September. The 2Y at 4.75% sits about 87bp above that.
    • My inference: the 2Y sits 87bp over funds, which, net of some term premium, still prices roughly one more hike. That is more than a 2% core PCE trend would justify — but the case depends on a soft September PCE confirming the cooling, not on declaring it already shown.
    • Supporting that the gap is too wide: hiring is sluggish (+29k payrolls in September, about 50k a month over three months; unemployment up to 4.2%), and the AI selloff is now pushing money into Treasuries.
    • The hot inflation is in headline CPI, from energy (+0.40% MoM in August, about 3.7% annualized since November). Inflation-swap-implied expectations (10Y breakeven 2.33%) treat that as noise.
    • Confirm: core CPI tomorrow at 0.25% or less, then a soft September PCE on Oct 29. That PCE lands the same morning the FOMC decides, so the Fed reads it before acting.
    • Refute: core at 0.35% or more and the 2Y back above 4.9%.
  • Credit is moving faster than stocks, but protection is still priced at an ordinary level.
    • High-yield spreads widened about 48bp over the month (to 3.15%, peaking at 3.24% on Oct 1), including 22bp since Sep 25. That move is larger than 90% of monthly moves in the past three years.
    • The level, though, is only at the 62nd percentile of that window, close to its 3.02% median. So you can buy insurance at a normal price after an unusual move.
    • The catalysts are converging. Memory chip stocks slid after a Korea selloff. The IMF flagged about $1.4tn of circular AI deals and the risk of a "disorderly" spike in bond yields. Stocks hovering near records are the outlier.
    • Refute: spreads back below 3.0%.

Key News

  • Early strain is showing at the most cyclical link of the AI trade. Memory names fell after the Korea selloff, and Bloomberg reports stock losses deepening on the AI trade while Treasuries rally. That is the cyclical, financing-dependent end of the build-out, not the cash-rich platforms.
  • The IMF issued a triple warning: a possible disorderly bond-yield spike, a possible AI stock crash, and $1.4tn of circular AI deals. It reinforces both the long-end term-premium view and the AI split.
  • Goldman's Q3 profit rose 29% on an equity-trading beat, while fixed income missed. Volatility is paying the trading desks. That is not a read on lending margins or credit quality, so the rest of this week's bank prints still matter.
  • Trump's diesel deal with Putin has drawn bipartisan and European pushback. WSJ says it won't ease fuel prices much. NATO's commander also warned Kyiv over its refinery strikes. Stay out of refiners. The hurricane trigger has passed, and the contested diesel deal is an added risk to margins if it holds, not an established cap.
  • NATO laid out a plan to defend Europe "with or without US forces," and Poland blamed Russia for Starlink sabotage. Both add a self-funded European layer to the defense restocking cycle.
  • Visa won Beijing's approval to prepare a domestic card-clearing business ahead of the Trump-Xi summit. My inference: China is offering market access as summit goodwill, which lowers the odds of a near-term US-China escalation (Xi out before 2027: 2.75%).
  • Six Flags closed nine rides after a fatality on its Georgia roller coaster. That is a direct revenue hit during the October season.

Macro

Rates & Fed

  • The FOMC meets Oct 28–29 per the catalyst calendar. That supersedes the Nov 4–5 date carried in prior notes. The decision lands the same day as Q3 GDP and September PCE, so Oct 29 is the key event of the month.
  • The curve is steeper but stalled. The 10Y–2Y spread is +44bp and the 10Y–3M about +99bp, both comfortably positive and nowhere near inversion. The 10Y at 5.22% is mostly real yield (about 2.9%) rather than inflation fear.
  • The term-premium view is intact. But Treasuries rallying on the AI selloff shows long bonds still act as a hedge against stocks. The front end gives the same risk-off rally without exposure to heavy bond issuance (supply), so I prefer it over the long end.
  • The midterm odds don't change the deficit path. Prediction markets give Democrats a 90.5% chance of the House and 61.5% of a full sweep, which implies gridlock from January 2027. My inference: no new tax cuts, but no deficit cuts either, so this neither adds to nor relieves long-end pressure.

Inflation (Sept CPI tomorrow)

  • Headline will run hot, driven by gasoline. Core is the print that matters.
    • Core CPI is 2.0% annualized over three months, but that is flattered by June's −0.02%. July plus August annualize to about 3.1%.
    • Core PCE, the Fed's gauge, is cooler over three months but still 3.0% year over year, with August re-accelerating to +0.25%. The September print on Oct 29 is the decisive read.
  • I resolve the CPI-vs-PCE gap in favor of PCE, because that is what the Fed targets.
  • The 10Y breakeven (2.33%) is flat against realized headline running about 3.7% annualized. The breakeven rightly looks through the energy-driven spike; short-dated TIPS accrue that realized inflation directly as tactical carry.

Labor & Consumer

  • Employers are neither hiring nor firing much. Continuing claims fell from 1.80M in late July to 1.72M, and job openings are 7.08M. The labor market is soft, not cracking, so there is no recession signal.
  • The consumer squeeze thesis is still fading. August retail sales rose 1.13% nominal, about +0.7% after inflation. Michigan sentiment at 51.7 is weak, but spending is winning that argument. September retail sales decide whether the thesis is dead.
  • Housing is soft: starts at 1,275k are below their 10-year median and choppy, with no recovery. Mortgage rates priced off a 10Y above 5.2% keep it that way.

Liquidity & Dollar

  • Financial conditions are tightening at the margin but remain loose. The Chicago Fed index moved from −0.551 to −0.494, still looser than average. M2 is growing about 6.4% annualized, which is the cushion under stocks.
  • The broad dollar is up 1.7% since Sep 21, driven by the Fed hike plus risk-off. That hurts multinationals' translated earnings and emerging-market assets.

Geopolitics

  • Oil is absorbing Gulf escalation without breaking out. WTI has traded $85–100 and sits around $96. The upstream view is unchanged. With no new trigger today, I keep a light upstream sleeve and no refiners.
  • The transatlantic rift is a demand driver for European defense. The diesel deal, NATO planning without the US, and Russian hybrid attacks all add to it.
  • Brazil: a Flávio Bolsonaro win is priced at 86.6%. That is mostly in the price, so there is no edge in Brazilian equities (EWZ).
  • Israel: Netanyahu-out odds are 41.5% by year-end, and oil hasn't reacted to that.

Sectors & Names

  • AI build-out vs. moats.
    • Underweight memory (MU), neoclouds and financing-dependent data-center names (IREN, CRWV), and ORCL. These are the first to reprice when credit widens. Memory's slide is an early signal of the order.
    • Hold the cash-rich consolidators (MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL).
    • The Oct 29 GDP equipment and software investment line is the hard check on AI capex.
  • Defense: overweight XAR, LMT, RTX, NOC, and add European exposure (e.g. EUAD) for the self-funded layer.
  • Financials: prefer trading and investment-banking houses (GS, MS) over regional banks (KRE). My inference: volatility pays trading desks, while regionals carry the cost of the hikes and commercial real estate exposure.
  • Housing: stay underweight homebuilders (ITB) and REITs (VNQ). That reverses only if the 2Y breaks 4.50%.
  • Energy: light upstream sleeve (XLE/XOP); avoid VLO, PSX and MPC.
  • Names:
    • Avoid FUN (Six Flags): ride closures hit peak October attendance.
    • V gets a modest positive from the China approval, but it's a watch item, not a position. The approval is preparatory and the summit timing isn't confirmed.
    • AAPL's event today is a watch item only, because foldable reports are contradictory: one says orders were raised 30%, another says sales will be "riddled with problems."

Positioning Summary

  • Overweight the 2Y; run a 2s10s steepener. Because the 2Y prices roughly one more hike net of term premium, while core PCE is still 3.0% year over year with a cooling trend that needs September's print to confirm. A risk-off move or soft core inflation squeezes that premium out, and the long leg stays exposed to term premium.
  • Underweight long duration (ZROZ) into the early-November refunding. Because the long end is mostly real yield (about 2.9%), which we think is being pushed up by supply and term premium, and the IMF warns of a disorderly spike. The refunding is the test.
  • Buy high-yield protection, or underweight HYG versus LQD. Because the spread level is still near its three-year median even after a 90th-percentile move.
  • Underweight AI build-out (MU, neoclouds, ORCL) versus overweight moats (MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL). Because build-out names depend on credit and early strain in credit is showing first at the cyclical end.
  • Overweight defense (XAR, LMT, RTX, NOC, plus European defense). Because demand is multi-theatre and Europe is adding self-funded spending.
  • Hold a small short-dated TIPS position (VTIP) through Oct 29 as a tactical carry play through the hot energy-driven headline prints. The breakeven rightly looks through it, so this is short-dated carry, not a call that breakevens are mispriced. Reassess after PCE.
  • Underweight ITB and VNQ; avoid refiners and FUN.

Key Tells

  • Core CPI tomorrow: 0.25% or less supports the 2Y overweight. 0.35% or more with the 2Y back above 4.9% means cut the front-end long and lean into the long-end underweight.
  • HY spread: above 3.5% before Oct 29 means de-risk equity beta broadly. Below 3.0% means close the protection.
  • Oct 29 PCE, GDP and FOMC: a soft core PCE plus a stalling capex line confirms both themes. A hawkish hike alongside soft PCE means the Fed is making a policy error, and the curve flattens.
  • Early-November refunding: a weak 30Y auction (one that clears at a higher-than-expected yield) confirms the long-end underweight. A strong one challenges it.
