Macro

Inflation

  • Core CPI has re-accelerated three months running. It rose +0.22% in July, +0.29% in August and +0.335% in September. Headline rose +0.65% in September on energy.
  • Core PCE has been softer. June–August annualizes to ~2.0% (August alone +0.25%). That makes tomorrow's September PCE the deciding print: either the gap with CPI closes, or the hawks' case weakens.
  • The breakeven curve is still inverted. The 5Y breakeven is 2.45% (76th percentile of 10 years, off its 2.48% peak); the 10Y is 2.35%. The market reads the inflation shock as front-loaded. That call still holds.

Labor & Growth

  • The labor market isn't cracking. Continuing claims are 1.73M, down from 1.80M in August. JOLTS openings are 7.08M.
  • But some demand is softening. Real retail sales were roughly flat in September after a solid Q3 (nominal +1.65% over three months). It's one soft month, not a stall. Starts are at 1.221M, 16th percentile of the last 10 years and ~12% below the H1 average.
  • Factories are the exception. Industrial production rose +0.66% in September.
  • How I square it: a supply-constrained economy. Low hiring is enough to hold unemployment down, so the Fed sees a "tight" labor market while consumers soften at the margin. This is inference, and the Nov 6 payrolls report is the test.

Rates & Credit

  • The 2Y fell 14bp in two weeks to 4.68%; the 10Y fell 21bp to 5.00%.
  • The curve is flattening gently, not heading for inversion. 10Y–2Y is +0.29, still well above zero.
  • The term-premium thesis remains on the back foot. I shelved it after the long-end rally; early-November refunding is the test.
  • HY spreads have backed off. They are 3.30% after peaking at 3.40%, against a 3.02% median for the last 3 years. They widened 37bp in a month, a bigger move than 83% of months. My 3.50% de-risk trigger was never hit, so I'm not pressing credit shorts.
  • Real 10Y yield is ~2.65% (5.00% nominal minus the 2.35% breakeven). That is a heavy discount-rate headwind for long-duration equities.
