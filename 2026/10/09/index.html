Daily Memo (09-Oct-2026)

Bottom line: The AI buildout crack is arriving ahead of schedule — OpenAI's revenue shortfall and a scrapped Nvidia-backed IPO are the first signals forcing the market to scrutinize AI monetization, and they land before the late-October mega-cap earnings test that was supposed to adjudicate the narrow rally. Meanwhile the front end is showing a marginal dovish lean while the long end stays supply-pinned. Stay in the steepener, hold the moats, press the buildout short.

Key Themes

  • The equity–AI reconciliation is cracking early, not at earnings. OpenAI's $70B annualized revenue is ~$20B below what it had signaled, and Nvidia-backed Firmus pulled a $5B data-center IPO on valuation concerns. These are the first revenue-expectation miss and a deferred IPO on valuation — not another circular-financing headline — and they hit the exact layer (application monetization, buildout funding) the capex is meant to justify. The crack thesis is strengthened but not yet adjudicated; the decisive test remains ahead. Confirm/refute: MSFT/AMZN/Alphabet capex + gross margins — raised capex with intact margins defends the complex; any margin warning or capex trim validates the crack. Note that mega-cap tech earnings dates in the Oct 22–30 window are unconfirmed; confirmed near-term reads are FOMC Oct 28 and Q3 GDP/PCE Oct 29.
  • The front end is showing a marginal dovish lean while the headline narrative is still "higher-for-longer." 2Y fell to 4.77% (from 4.84%) on a +29k payroll print and U-rate ticking back up to 4.2% — a 7bp move that is not yet pricing cuts, but is the first marginal lean toward a labor cool-down even as the long end holds ~5.28% on supply. The steepener is intact, driven by term premium, with the front-end easing bid a watch-item rather than a confirmed driver. Oct 14 CPI is the referee: a soft core flips the pause-durable debate toward cuts.
  • The consumer-squeeze thesis is losing the argument, but not buried. August retail +1.13% MoM nominal against +0.40% CPI = ~+0.7% real — one firm print after a real-negative July, a choppy series rather than a confirmed streak. Demand is holding despite Michigan sentiment at 51.7. This is fading as a short thesis — don't press discretionary shorts on a stagflation narrative the real spending data keeps rebutting, but wait for a third real-positive print before declaring the squeeze thesis finished.
