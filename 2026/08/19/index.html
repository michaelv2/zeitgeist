Key Themes

  • The long end, not the Fed, is where the risk is repricing. "Bond jitters" are showing up as curve steepening, not cut repricing: 10Y–2Y at 0.53 is a fresh multi-month cycle high (up from 0.44 two weeks ago) and 10Y–3M at 0.85, while the 2Y sits anchored at 4.19 (~55bp over the 3.63% funds rate). That's term-premium rebuild at the back end, not a dovish front-end move — it validates the ZROZ underweight into the Nov 5 refunding. Confirm: further 10Y–2Y widening / long-bond selloff. Refute: 2Y breaking below ~3.75 (cut trade genuinely reviving).
  • Canada tariff pause tempts the market to fade the cost-push thesis — don't. The 50% Canada tariff is paused three days on a "near" deal, and a headline reader will extrapolate broad de-escalation. But tariff passthrough to Q4 core prints is regime-wide, and core CPI is still firming (+0.22% MoM July, ~2.6% annualized). One bilateral pause doesn't disarm the channel. The Sept 11 August CPI is the adjudicator — ≥+0.4% MoM revives the duration UW; ≤+0.2% reopens the cut door.
  • A Middle East regime tail is quietly building. Israel struck a Syrian airbase over explicit US objection as the US-Netanyahu rift widens, and the market prices Netanyahu out by year-end at a coin-flip (47.5%). Combined with the Korea drill cuts straining alliances, this reinforces the defense-restocking OW and keeps small energy convexity live.
