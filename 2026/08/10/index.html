Daily Memo (10-Aug-2026)

Key Themes

  • The labor crack is printing — and the tape hasn't fully absorbed it. July payrolls fell 23k (158.858M vs 158.881M in June), the first outright monthly decline since February 2026. The unemployment drop to 4.1% (from 4.2%) is ambiguous and possibly participation-driven — the household-survey breakdown is needed to know whether it reflects labor-force exit or genuine hiring. Weight of evidence says labor is softening, which partially confirms the dovish leg: the payrolls leg of the prior "sub-50k crack" tell tripped, but the U-rate fell rather than rising above 4.2% and continuing claims (1,801k) remain below the 1.85M threshold — so this is a first crack that September 4 payrolls must corroborate, not a fully confirmed dovish leg. It still underwrites a Sept cut and keeps the front-end bid. What kills it: a large upward revision to July or a firm August payrolls (Sept 4).
  • The melt-up now has its fuel. JPM lifted its 2026 S&P target to 8,000 and Kalshi is pricing it as likely — and a dovish labor pivot plus expanding liquidity (M2 +$980B since Sept to $23.16T) is exactly the accelerant. But the same euphoria (OpenAI IPO markets clustering $750B–$1.25T) is the fragility: it sets an unpriced valuation anchor for the whole high-multiple AI cohort when it prints. Stay barbelled, don't chase the index leg.
  • Disinflation is a near-term cushion for the consumer. June headline CPI fell -0.42% MoM, core flat, core PCE +0.13% (~1.6% annualized). That makes June retail sales (+0.22% nominal) real-positive (~+0.6%) — but that real gain is largely a function of falling energy prices, with core flat; it is a near-term cushion, not an underappreciated structural support. Real incomes are not being squeezed in the immediate term, and it supports both the consumer and the cut path, but the durability depends on whether disinflation holds beyond energy.

Macro

Rates & Yields

  • Front-end add in play; bull-steepening cut narrative intact. 2Y at 4.25 (Aug 6, pre-payroll) but the negative July print should press it back toward/below 4.20. 10Y 4.69, curve +0.44/0.46 — steepening on cuts, not term-premium blowout.
  • Fed is restrictive and has room. Funds at 3.63% against core PCE running ~1.6% annualized — real policy rate is tight into a softening labor market. Sept 15–16 FOMC is the delivery date.
  • Long end still underweight. Bull-steepener favors belly over ZROZ; the +0.46 spread is a cut trade, not a duration-rally trade.

Labor

  • Softening, but the composite crack tell is not fully tripped. Payrolls -23k, continuing claims drifting up (1,801k), JOLTS off peak (7,359k). The payrolls leg of the crack tell has tripped; the 4.1% U-rate and 1,801k claims remain below their respective thresholds. The U-rate drop is ambiguous — possibly participation-driven, but the household-survey breakdown is needed to confirm.

Inflation

  • Disinflation resumption is the live base case (June core flat, core PCE +0.13%, WTI ~$82 removing the energy amplifier), pending July CPI (Aug 14) as the adjudicator. TIPS stay underweight. The solar/tariff pass-through is the only live upside risk.

Consumer & USD

  • Consumer holding in real terms, near-term (Michigan bounced to 49.5 off the 44.8 low; real retail positive on energy tailwind). The real-positive retail read is largely energy-driven with core flat — a near-term cushion, not a structural call. Cheaper gas is a real-income support for now.
  • Dollar drifting weaker, orderly (broad USD 119.7 from 120.9). Clean tailwind for gold, commodities, multinationals — no vol spike, so the yen-carry tail stays latent, not active.

  • Oil premium draining despite the Hormuz stalemate. Two-sided but I lean fade: Iran holding firm keeps a floor (~$80s), but a hollowed US arsenal and stalled talks argue against escalation-to-blockade. Energy = neutral/fade-the-spike, not re-arm.

  • AI — stay barbelled. Long memory/HBM (Apple's ~40% cost inflation is a demand tell for DRAM/HBM) and margin-defended moats; underweight vendor-financed, capex-heavy names. The OpenAI IPO is a cohort-wide re-rating risk if it prints ≥$1T.

  • Crypto divergence worth noting: BTC $150k by year-end priced at only 3.6% — crypto is not participating in the equity melt-up. If the risk-on/liquidity thesis is right, that's either a laggard to catch up or a signal the melt-up is narrower than the index target implies.

Positioning Summary

  • Overweight: defense (XAR/primes), gold (IAU), AI moats + memory/HBM.
  • Add: front-end/belly duration on the labor softening signal (cuts into Sept FOMC, pending Aug 4 payrolls corroboration).
  • Underweight: long-duration Treasuries (ZROZ), TIPS, homebuilders, vendor-financed AI capex names.

  • Hedge, don't chase: the index melt-up leg — keep a light equity-vol hedge against the OpenAI-IPO/AI-multiple overhang; USD weakness tilts toward multinationals over domestics.
