Midterms are tomorrow, but the bigger risk this week is Iran. The 2Y has fallen 20bp in a week while the Fed still looks set to hike in December, so I'd fade the front-end rally. I'd also buy index protection now: as of Oct 29, VIX was around its 10-year median (17.2, 52nd percentile), which is not rich given stacked binaries — check where vol is after Friday before sizing the put spreads.

Key Themes

  • The Iran decision after the midterms matters more than the midterms, and options aren't pricing it.
    • A Democratic sweep is already about 72% priced (House 94.5%). The US separately warned allies it would strike Iran after Nov 3.
    • Oil is retreating because a draft deal is circulating in Doha. But Iran's negotiators have left, and both sides are still threatening to resume fighting.
    • VIX was 17.2 on Oct 29, right at its 10-year median, and that was before Friday's AI selloff. High-yield spreads were 3.26%. So the market is pricing a normal week when oil could move sharply either way.
    • Would confirm: a strike, or the talks collapsing. Would refute: a signed deal with WTI holding below $90.
  • A weak October payrolls number this week is likely to be misread as dovish.
    • Payrolls averaged only 68k a month from December to September (September +29k). Yet unemployment is 4.2% and continuing claims fell to 1.70M.
    • That points to tight labor supply (the deportation market centers on 400–500k), not weak demand. My inference: the payroll level that keeps unemployment flat is now very low.
    • The unemployment rate is the number that matters, not the headline jobs count. Core CPI is running ~3.4% annualized over three months and headline CPI rose 0.65% in September. Together those argue the December hike stays live, whatever the bond rally implies.
