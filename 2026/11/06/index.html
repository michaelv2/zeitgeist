Daily Memo (06-Nov-2026)

Bottom line: The market is treating the Democratic sweep as an AI growth shock and an end to Fed hikes. I think both reads are overdone. A federal data-center ban cannot become law while Trump can veto it, and inflation is still running too hot for the Fed to step in. The trades: buy short-dated TIPS (STIP) after a sharp 20bp drop in breakevens, stay out of the 2-year Treasury, and use the indiscriminate AI selloff to own data centers that already have power, against developers that still have to build.

Key Themes

  • The market is pricing a federal data-center crackdown that cannot pass before 2029.
    • Top Democrats are calling for a "national data center moratorium now," and roughly $2.5T came out of tech in one session.
    • But the new House (~250 Democratic seats) is far short of the 290 needed to override a veto, and the Senate is far short of 67. The prediction market gives an AI safety law before 2027 only 8.5%.
    • The real channels are state permitting, state utility rate cases and congressional oversight. All three hurt new builds and raise the scarcity value of capacity that is already powered. A federal ban is a 2029 question (Democrats are 67.5% to win the 2028 presidency). That hurts builders whose projects come online in 2029 or later, not today's incumbents.
    • What would prove this wrong: a moratorium attached to must-pass appropriations, or Trump himself turning against data centers.
  • The front end of the curve is pricing relief that inflation doesn't support.
    • 5-year breakevens fell 20bp in four sessions to 2.26%. The 2-year was at 4.58% on Nov 4, and my estimate from the curve is about 4.50–4.52% on Nov 5, right at our 4.50% no-backstop test. A close clearly below 4.50% that holds through Nov 10 CPI would mean the no-Fed-put call is wrong.
    • Meanwhile core CPI is running at 3.4% annualized over three months, headline at about 4.6%, and unemployment is 4.2%.
    • A wealth shock in tech stocks is not a labor shock. The market still gives a December hike 68% (down from ~75%), and I think the hike happens.
    • A second-order effect, my inference: a Democratic Senate can block dovish Fed nominees, which makes the hawkish path more credible, not less.
  • Weak payrolls are no longer a sign of slack. Low unemployment alongside ~68k/month payrolls is consistent with slower labor-supply growth (my inference; deportation odds suggest a meaningful drag). A soft Dec 4 payroll print isn't dovish unless unemployment rises with it. Job openings rose to 7.52M and continuing claims are 1.72M.

Macro

Fed & Rates

  • The Fed is still hiking. The effective funds rate is 3.88%, up from 3.63% before September. The 2-year about 70bp above it still prices a December hike plus more in 2027, and that is correct.
  • Fade the 2-year rally. Keep front-end cash in T-bills or floating-rate Treasuries (USFR) rather than extending to 2 years. A core CPI print of 0.30% or more on Nov 10 should reverse the move.
  • The October jobs report moved to Dec 4, so last memo's Nov 6 test is gone. Nov 10 CPI is the next big test. Core at 0.30%+ makes the December hike the strong base case, and Dec 4 unemployment and Dec 10 CPI can still change it.
  • The term-premium short thesis is closed. The 10-year is at 4.90% and the market gives a 5.5% 10-year only 4.6%. The curve steepened slightly as yields fell (10s/2s at +38bp), nowhere near inverting. If you hold duration, hold it in 7–10 years (IEF), not long zeros (ZROZ).

Inflation & TIPS

  • September was hot on both measures. Headline CPI rose 0.65% and core 0.33%; core PCE rose 0.32% (2.9% annualized over three months).
  • Add STIP. The 5-year breakeven fell 20bp in four sessions to 2.26%, roughly its 10-year median. That is about 1.5pt below headline CPI YoY (3.7%) and about 1.1pt below 3-month core (3.4%). Short TIPS still accrue realized CPI above that.
    • Part of the drop is justified: WTI fell from $107.56 to $99.64. But core inflation at 3.4% doesn't depend on oil.
    • What the market is pricing is a risk-off growth scare, not disinflation. Short TIPS accrue realized CPI either way.
  • Trim only if October headline CPI prints 0.2% or less. October's high oil prices make that unlikely. Still avoid long TIPS (TIPZ).

Consumer & Housing

  • Real spending stalled in September. Retail sales rose 0.77% against a 0.65% CPI gain, so real growth was about 0.1%.
  • Sentiment is near its lows (Michigan 48.1), and the tech-wealth hit lands on high-end spenders next.
  • Housing is rolling over. Starts fell for a third straight month to 1,221k, the 16th percentile of the last decade and about 11% below the Dec–Feb average. A 10-year near 4.9% keeps mortgage rates pinned (the market gives a 5.9% low by year-end only 4.5%). Underweight homebuilders.
  • This is not recession pricing. Recession odds are 4.5%, 2026 GDP growth of 2.0% or more is about 99%, and industrial production rose 0.66% in September.

Credit & Volatility

  • These readings predate the rout. On Nov 4, high-yield spreads tightened to 3.32% and the VIX fell to 16.6 (post-election relief). Neither captures Nov 5.
  • HY at 3.32% (Nov 4) is above its 3-year median of 3.02% (82nd percentile of a short 3-year window). The +59bp three-month widening is large (84th percentile of moves), but the level is still tight historically and 18bp below the 3.50% trigger.
  • Hedging plan:
    • Take profits on half the QQQ put spreads into today's volatility spike.
    • Hold the SPY puts through CPI.
    • High-yield spreads above 3.50% means cut equity beta and press HYG puts.
  • No Fed put. The Fed will not rescue tech multiples with core inflation running at 3.4%.
