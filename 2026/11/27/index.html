Daily Memo (27-Nov-2026)

Bottom line: Today's AI-led futures rally is not confirmed by credit, the dollar or real yields. High-yield spreads were 3.72% as of the last print (Nov 24), the dollar is at multi-week highs, and 10Y real yields sit at the 99th percentile of the last decade. Keep the equity hedges on. The oil slide will make November headline CPI look soft and could fool the market, because core inflation is still running near 3.7% annualized. Two prior calls change today: exit the rest of XLE, and cut the curve steepener to half.

Key Themes

  • Equities are rallying on AI while credit keeps widening.
    • High-yield spreads are 3.72% (Nov 24). That is the 95th percentile of the last 3 years, and the +103bp move over 3 months is larger than 95% of past 3-month moves. Credit had not confirmed the bounce as of the last print. Today's close is the real test, and a move back toward 3.50% would weaken the hedge case.
    • The prediction market now prices only a 15.7% chance the "AI bubble bursts" in 2026. Equity holders have relaxed; credit holders have not. When the two disagree this much, credit has usually been the better guide (that is my inference, not a tested rule).
    • Call: keep the full SPY/QQQ puts. Don't monetize them into the bounce.
    • What would change it: high-yield back below 3.20% = reassess. 4.00% = take profits on a third of the puts and roll strikes down.
  • A soft November CPI headline on Dec 10 could be read as disinflation when it isn't.
    • WTI fell about 11% from Nov 12 to Nov 24 ($92.30 → $82.24). That should pull November headline CPI down through gasoline.
    • Underneath, core is not cooling. October core CPI rose 0.30% MoM (3.7% annualized over 3 months) and core PCE rose 0.29% (3.6%).
    • Markets price inflation as if it were tame: the 5Y breakeven is 2.13%, below its 10-year median of 2.21%. A hiking Fed facing core near 3.7% is not consistent with that.
    • Expressions: inflation protection (TIPs) is cheap. Any rally in long-duration bonds on the soft headline should be faded.
  • Slow payroll growth no longer signals a weak labor market.
    • Payrolls averaged only about 78k a month in January–October, yet unemployment fell from 4.4% to 4.1%.
    • The prediction market gives 62% odds that deportations land in the 400–500k range. That points to shrinking labor supply, which lowers the pace of hiring needed to keep unemployment stable (my inference).
    • So a low payroll number on Dec 4 is weak evidence of a slowdown. The unemployment rate is the signal to watch. This weakens the "growth scare" leg of the front-end trade (see Rates).

Macro

Inflation & Fed

  • The December hike is settled; the open question is 2027.
    • The prediction market gives 93.6% odds of a 4.25% upper bound at year-end and 98.5% odds of no cuts in 2026.
    • Core PCE at about 3.6% annualized against an effective fed funds rate of 3.88% means the real policy rate is only about +0.3%. The policy rate itself is not very restrictive.
    • Most of the tightening is coming from the long end: 10Y real yield of 2.79% (99th percentile of 10 years, near the 2.95% decade high). That is my inference.
  • Headline inflation is running hotter than core but is about to turn. Headline CPI rose about 5.7% annualized over the 3 months to October, driven by energy. With WTI now at $82, November headline should undershoot core.

Labor

  • The labor market is holding up.
    • October payrolls +165k; unemployment 4.1%; job openings 7.52M in September, up from August.
    • Continuing claims spiked to 1.792M (Nov 7) and then fell straight back to 1.697M. Treat that spike as noise.
  • New tell for Dec 4: unemployment at 4.3% or higher means real slack. Payrolls below 75k with unemployment at 4.1% or lower is a supply story and is not dovish.

Consumer & Housing

    • Retail sales rose 0.21% nominal against a 0.35% rise in CPI in October. Over 3 months, however, nominal retail sales rose 2.5% (Jul→Oct) against 1.4% headline CPI.
    • Michigan sentiment was 48.0, near the 2026 low of 44.8.
    • Industrial production fell 0.4%.
  • Retail call: underweight XRT.
    • Deep Black Friday discounting is a margin risk for Q4 retailer results. This is a mechanism-only bet on Black Friday margin compression, not a call backed by a real-spending stall.
    • This rests on the mechanism. My inputs contain no retailer pricing or estimates to measure what is already priced.
  • Housing is stuck while the 10Y is near 4.9%.
    • Housing starts are 1.31M, down from 1.52M in March. Case-Shiller is up only about 1.1% over 9 months, which is negative after inflation.
    • The prediction market gives only 4% odds that 30-year mortgage rates touch 5.90% this year.
    • Avoid XHB and VNQ while real yields sit at decade highs.
