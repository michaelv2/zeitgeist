Daily Memo (04-Nov-2026)

Bottom line: The Democratic sweep leaves Washington gridlocked, but it gives Democrats real power in the states, and the market's first reaction (selling every AI/data-center name) points at the right place for the wrong reasons. The bigger risk is unchanged and getting closer: the Fed looks set to hike in December (~75% priced) while credit spreads are already widening. Weak payroll numbers shouldn't stop it unless unemployment rises, because unemployment is low and falling labor supply, not falling demand, looks like what's holding hiring down so far.

Key Themes

  • The midterm risk that matters is state permitting, not federal law. If Texas permitting tightens, existing powered capacity becomes scarcer and more valuable. Federal legislation is effectively dead: Trump keeps the veto, and the markets give only 9% to a corporate tax cut and 11% to a capital-gains cut. But the Texas governor flip (Hinojosa) and Democrats campaigning on data centers turn permits and power-price politics into the live channel. The market is selling "AI data centers" as one basket. My read (inference): limits on new building raise the value of capacity that is already powered and running, and hurt new-build developers. This is a starter position until the NVDA/hyperscaler confirm condition or Texas bill language arrives.
    • Trade: long incumbent data-center landlords (EQIX, DLR) vs. neoclouds with Texas build pipelines (CRWV, IREN). Underweight power producers whose story depends on Texas grid (ERCOT) demand growth (VST, NRG).
    • Confirm: NVDA (Nov 17) or hyperscalers cite power or site limits as the bottleneck.
    • Kill: Texas moratorium talk fades without bill language.
  • The Fed is hiking into a credit selloff, and the bond market's front end is moving the other way. December hike odds rose to ~75% from 64%. Yet the 2Y fell 22bp (4.81% to 4.59%), the VIX jumped to 21.2 (73rd percentile of the last 10 years) from 17.2, and the high-yield spread hit 3.43%, 7bp from my 3.50% de-risk trigger.
    • My read: the 2Y rally is investors buying safety plus pricing in gridlock, not a Fed pivot. September core CPI was +0.335%, continuing claims are at 1.70M (the low of the recent window), and job openings are at 7.52M. So the Fed follows through and credit takes the strain.
    • What would make me wrong: October unemployment at 4.4% or higher on Nov 6.

Macro

Inflation & Fed

  • Inflation is re-accelerating, not just being pushed up by energy. September CPI rose +0.65% headline and +0.335% core, the third straight faster core print. Core CPI is running ~3.4% annualized over three months and core PCE ~2.9%. Headline CPI is up 3.15% from December to September, about 4.2% annualized.
  • A September hike is already in. Monthly average fed funds went 3.63% (Aug), 3.75% (Sep), 3.88% (Oct). Markets give ~75% to a 4.25% upper bound by year-end and 97% to no cuts in 2026.
  • The 2Y at 4.59% still sits ~70bp above effective funds, so it prices December plus more hikes in 2027. The rally trimmed how high the market thinks rates go; it did not price a rescue.

Labor & Growth

  • Weak payrolls look more like a labor-supply drag than a demand drop so far: unemployment has stayed in a 4.1–4.4% range all year and continuing claims are flat near 1.70M. Nov 6 tests this. September added just +29k jobs (about 68k a month since December). But unemployment is 4.2%, claims are falling and openings are rising. The deportation market's most likely bucket is 400–500k (58%).
    • Nov 6: judge by the unemployment rate. At 4.2% or lower, the hike stays on whatever the payroll number says.
  • No recession in sight. Industrial production rose +0.66% in September, and recession odds are 5.5%.

Rates

  • I'm dropping the view that bond supply would drive a long-end selloff. The sweep removes the tax-cut route to bigger deficits. The 10Y is down to 4.91% from 5.13%, and the market gives only 11% to a 10Y at 5.5%. The Treasury refunding (early November) is the last test: longer issuance or weak auctions would revive it.
  • Expect bear flattening: Fed hikes lift the 2Y while a lower term premium under gridlock holds the 10Y roughly steady. That's why any duration belongs in 7–10Y rather than the 2Y. 10Y–2Y is +34bp, inside a 29–38bp range and nowhere near inverting.
    • Positioning: hold any duration in 7–10Y (IEF), not the 2Y. Stay in bills for the front end into Nov 6 and Nov 10.
    • Why it should pay: the front end has rallied even as hike odds rose. That gap closes if core prints at 0.30% or more.
  • Keep owning short-dated TIPS (STIP/VTIP), but change the trim trigger. The 5Y breakeven is 2.39%, down from 2.46%, but that move is small (smaller than 92% of past one-month moves). Short TIPS earn realized CPI, which is running near 4% annualized, against a 2.39% breakeven. Since that accrual drives the return, trim on a soft October headline CPI (0.2% or less), not on the 2.35% breakeven level. Still avoid long TIPS (TIPZ) and long zeros (ZROZ).

Credit & Vol

  • My equity hedges have paid off, so I'm not adding at a VIX of 21. SPY/QQQ put spreads bought near a VIX of 17 are working. Hold them through the Nov 10 CPI.
  • Credit is where to watch. The high-yield spread is 3.43%, up 33bp in a month (87th percentile of the last 3 years; short history, so weak evidence). Above 3.50%, cut equity beta and add HYG puts.
  • The discount-rate headwind is still heavy: the 10Y real yield is ~2.62% (4.91% nominal minus a 2.29% breakeven).

Consumer & Housing

  • September retail sales (+0.77%) roughly matched headline CPI (+0.65%), so real goods spending was about flat that month. That's one print, inflated on both sides by gasoline. Michigan sentiment is 48.1. I'm underweight discretionary vs. staples, a call that rests on oil above $100 acting as a tax on consumers rather than on a measured slowdown in spending.
  • Housing starts are 1,221k, down 20% from March, while prices still edge up (Case-Shiller 334.2). Builders face Fed hikes and a 10Y near 4.9%. That's a reason to avoid homebuilders (ITB), not a short.
