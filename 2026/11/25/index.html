Daily Memo (25-Nov-2026)

Bottom line: The market has stopped treating this as a rates problem and is now pricing a growth and AI-credit problem. The Fed is still about 93% priced to hike in December anyway. I think the December hike is the last one, and that core inflation stays sticky after it. That supports owning the front end of the curve and cheap breakevens. The Nvidia story has moved from political risk toward enforcement risk — sanctions and blacklistings have reached the China diversion chain, and the pardon comment raises the temperature — which trips our tell to take that pair trade to full size.

Key Themes

  • The Fed will stop because growth and credit crack, not because inflation falls. Neither the front end nor breakevens is priced for that.
    • The market is pricing a disinflationary growth scare:
      • The 5Y breakeven fell to 2.11%. That is down 37bp in a month, a 96th-percentile move over the last 10 years, and below its 10-year median of 2.21%.
      • The 2Y fell 25bp from its peak (4.88% to 4.63%).
      • The HY spread hit 3.76% on Nov 20 (3.63% on Nov 23), the 94th percentile of the last 3 years.
    • But core inflation is not falling. October core CPI rose 0.30% MoM, about 3.7% annualized over 3 months.
    • My view: the Fed hikes in December (92.6% odds of a 4.25% upper bound), then holds into weakening growth while core runs around 3.5%. That outcome pays two trades:
      • Long the front end. After a December hike the effective rate is about 4.13%, so a 2Y at 4.63% still embeds roughly 50bp of further tightening (approximate, since the 2Y also carries term premium).
      • Long breakevens via long TIP / short IEF. At 2.11%, five-year inflation compensation sits well below the run-rate of core prices.
    • The two legs partly hedge each other. Hot core data hurts the front-end long but helps breakevens.
    • Refute: core PCE on Nov 27 at 0.35% or more, plus early-Dec core CPI at 0.35% or more, means the Fed keeps hiking. In that case, cut the front-end leg.
  • Enforcement has reached the China diversion chain, and that is not just a headline.
    • Treasury sanctioned Megaspeed. An Alibaba-owned firm was blacklisted. Reuters traced $270M of AI servers in China to firms linked to Alice Huang.
    • Trump says a Huang pardon is "always an option." Enforcement has reached the China diversion chain (sanctions, blacklistings), and the pardon comment raises the temperature, but no legal action against Nvidia has been reported.
    • Separately, there is a 24.75% market-implied chance of a US government stake in NVDA, an unpriced governance and dilution overhang.
    • All of this lands before Huang testifies to Congress, reportedly next week; the committee date is still pending.

Macro

Rates & Yields

  • This is a bull move at both ends, not yet a bull steepener. The 2Y fell 25bp and the 10Y fell 23bp (5.15% to 4.92%), so 2s10s has held at 28–29bp.
    • Keep the steepener. Its edge is unchanged: the front end still prices hikes that growth won't support.
    • Stop if 2s10s falls below 20bp.
  • 10Y–3M is the curve to watch. It compressed from 83bp to 55bp in under two weeks, because bills are pricing the December hike while the long end rallies.
    • It is not close to inverting, but at this pace a hike into falling long yields gets it there in weeks.
    • An inversion would revive the recession narrative that prediction markets price at only 2.65%. That market only covers the five weeks left in 2026, so it is low partly by construction.
  • Real yields have eased but remain extreme. The 10Y real yield is 2.79%, down from 2.92% and still at the 99th percentile of the last 10 years.
    • Long-duration equities have had little discount-rate relief.
    • I still avoid ZROZ. Prediction markets give 33.5% odds of $41T in federal debt before 2027, and a gridlocked government will not consolidate the budget.

Inflation

  • Breakevens are tracking oil, not core inflation.
    • Core PCE rose 0.32% in September and about 2.9% annualized over 3 months. October core CPI rose 0.30%.
    • Outside the US, prediction markets put Eurozone 2026 inflation at 3.1% or higher at 92% odds. Global inflation is not cooling.
  • Hold long TIP / short IEF. It is offside, but its edge is now bigger than when it was opened.
    • The 2.10% breakeven threshold for re-entering outright TIPS is one tick away.
    • Add outright TIP if Nov 27 core PCE prints 0.25% or more while the 5Y breakeven is below 2.10%.
    • Close if early-Dec core CPI is 0.20% or less.

Labor

  • Continuing claims jumped 70k to 1.763M (Nov 7) out of a nine-week range. That is one week and still below the 10-year median, so watch for follow-through.
    • One week falls in a holiday-distorted window, so it needs confirmation.
    • October payrolls rose 165k, but the January–October average is about 78k per month. Unemployment is 4.1%.
  • My inference: deportations are holding the unemployment rate down. Markets put 400–500k deportations at 61% odds. A smaller labor force keeps unemployment low even as hiring slows, so the unemployment rate overstates labor-market strength.
  • Dec 4 tell: unemployment at 4.3% or higher, or payrolls under 75k, confirms the claims rise is real and makes December the last hike.

  • Confounder: mid-December November retail sales may get a one-off GTA VI boost. Day-one sales of $3.79B are global, but equal about 0.5% of a month of US retail sales. Read through a strong headline before treating it as a demand recovery. This is inference.
