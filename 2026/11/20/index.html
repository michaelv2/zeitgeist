Daily Memo (20-Nov-2026)

Bottom line: The AI rout is testing credit. Anthropic opened at $1.8T and then sold off, and high-yield spreads are 3bp from our hedge trigger. At the same time the Fed is about to hike into a consumer whose October spending dipped after a strong three-month run. I'm reopening equity protection now and betting that the 2027 hiking path priced into the front end does not survive the tightening already underway.

Key Themes

  • The AI trade has lost both of its supports, and credit is starting to price it. The Anthropic IPO has turned from a question of how much new stock the market can absorb into a test of what AI companies are worth. Anthropic debuted at $1.8T, three times the $600B bar the market had priced, then fell. Asian markets are heading for their worst week since 2020. The Fed won't cushion this: markets give 98% odds of no cut in 2026 and 91% to a 4.25% upper bound by year-end. Real yields are near their decade high (10Y TIPS 2.88%, 99th percentile; the high is 2.95%). HY has given back its early-November tightening (3.20%→3.47%, back to the Nov 5 level). The 3-month widening is large for the last 3 years, but credit is not yet pricing an AI-specific break. The test is a close at or above 3.50%. The market gives 93% odds Apple is the largest company on Dec 31, consistent with low-capex megacaps as the relative haven.

    • Confirm: HY closes at or above 3.50%, or Q3 corporate profits are weak on Nov 25. Refute: HY back below 3.20% while real yields hold.

  • The front end prices more 2027 tightening than this economy can take. The 2Y yield is 4.80% against roughly 3.9% effective fed funds. That is about 70bp above where funds should sit after a December hike, which implies a multi-hike 2027. That is my inference, not a quoted futures strip. Meanwhile:

    • October real retail sales dipped after a strong three-month run (nominal +2.5% over 3 months, ahead of headline CPI). One soft month, not yet shrinkage. The Dec 15 print tests whether it persists.
    • Industrial production fell 0.4%.
    • Michigan sentiment is 48.1.
    • The AI selloff is now a wealth shock on top of that.

    The December hike happens; my call is that the 2027 hikes get priced out. The 2s10s curve at 27bp has flattened from 35bp and prices no growth damage.

    • Refute: November core CPI at 0.35% or higher on Dec 10.

Macro

    • Core CPI rose 0.30% in October, about 3.7% annualized over three months against 2.85% annualized from January to October.

    • Core PCE is steady at about 2.9% annualized.

Labor & Consumer

  • The labor market is firm enough for a hike. Payrolls rose 165k in October (three-month average 137k), unemployment is 4.1%, and JOLTS openings are 7.52M.
  • The jump in continuing claims is noise, not a crack. Claims rose to 1.763M, but the one-month move is only 62nd percentile and the level is 32nd percentile of the decade.
  • Consumers are being squeezed, not spending freely. October real retail sales dipped after a strong three-month run (nominal +2.5% over 3 months, ahead of headline CPI). One soft month, not yet shrinkage. Michigan sentiment is 48.1. GTA VI's roughly $2.3bn launch is spending on one title, not a sign of broad demand.

It loses on any flattening: a hot Dec 10 CPI (bear flattener), or a flight to quality that rallies the long end first, which is what happened Nov 5–12. Term-premium widening is a low-probability bonus (10.5% odds of the 10Y reaching 5.5%), not a core support. Prefer it to outright long duration (avoid ZROZ).
