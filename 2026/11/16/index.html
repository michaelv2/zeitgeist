Daily Memo (16-Nov-2026)

Key Themes

  • The bond selloff is all real yields, with almost no inflation premium. Own TIPS over nominal Treasuries.
    • The 10Y real yield hit 2.86%, near the top of its 10-year range (high 2.95%). It rose 20bp in two sessions and 44bp in three months, a bigger 3-month move than ~80% of the past decade's.
    • The 5Y breakeven, the market's expected inflation rate, is 2.30%. That is ordinary for the last decade (58th percentile) and sits below the 3.7% three-month annualized pace of core CPI.
    • So the market assumes the Fed has already won. The data doesn't support that: core PCE is not cooling. It has run steadily at about 2.9% annualized over three and six months, and 3.1% year-over-year, well above the 2.3% 5Y breakeven.
    • TIPS beat nominal Treasuries if core stays hot (breakevens widen). Outright TIPS gain if hikes get priced out (real yields fall). The risk is real yields grinding higher with breakevens flat. Size the outright exposure to that risk, or express it as TIPS vs IEF.
    • This replaces the IEF add flagged last week for when the 10Y hit 5% (it is now 5.10%). Plain Treasuries win only if inflation cools, and the core PCE trend says it isn't cooling.
    • What would prove this wrong: real yields keep rising while breakevens fall at the Dec 10 CPI.
  • Credit has eased back from its Nov 5 wide but is still about 50bp wider than three months ago and above its 3-year median. It is not confirming further stress this week. Close the SPY puts; the hedge trigger is off.
    • High-yield spreads tightened to 3.20% from 3.48% on Nov 5, even as real yields spiked. That trips last week's 3.30% trigger.
    • Close the SPY puts. Express the risk through relative positions in long-duration assets: rate-sensitive REITs, homebuilders and the most expensive AI stocks.
    • Prediction markets price a 2026 recession at only 2.45% and a >2.5% GDP year at 50%. Credit is on the right side of that.
  • Nvidia is now a governance and revenue-quality story, driven by the administration rather than the House.
    • Jensen Huang is out as CEO. Treasury Secretary Bessent says Nvidia must hire a CEO from outside. Nvidia has been ordered to explain $12bn of AI chip supply to China, and Bessent has publicly asked whether that "goosed" earnings.
    • Prediction markets already give Apple 91% odds of being the largest company at year-end (up from 73%) and Nvidia 7.5%. So the loss of market-cap leadership is priced; the risk of revenue being questioned is not.
    • This week's print can't clear a Treasury inquiry that has no deadline.

Macro

Rates & Inflation

  • A December hike is a given; the question is how far 2027 goes.
    • Odds of a 4.25% year-end upper bound are 88% (from 84%).
    • The 2Y yield rose 30bp in two weeks to 4.86%, about 100bp above the 3.88% effective funds rate. That prices the December hike plus roughly three more in 2027.
  • Inflation has broadened, and lower oil won't fix the core.
    • October core CPI rose 0.30% and headline 0.35%.
    • Falling oil will cool headline CPI on Dec 10. It doesn't help a core PCE trend that is not cooling.
  • Breakevens: keep STIP. Adding TIP/SCHP is the main call above. Avoid ZROZ: divided government limits new borrowing, but real yields near decade highs can still rise further at the long end.
  • Gold: don't add IAU. Real yields near their 10-year high are the clearest headwind, though gold has partly decoupled from real yields before (inference).

Labor & Growth

  • The economy is firm, which backs the hawks.
    • Payrolls rose 165k in October (three-month average about 137k). Unemployment is 4.1%, continuing claims 1.70M and flat, and job openings rose to 7.52M.
    • Industrial production rose 0.66% in September.
  • Consumer demand is real but thin.
    • Nominal September retail sales rose 0.77% against a 0.65% rise in CPI, so real growth was about +0.1%.
    • Michigan sentiment is 48.1. The oil drop is the near-term relief for real incomes.
  • The deportation range of 400–500k (61.5% odds) keeps labor supply tight (inference). That argues against expecting unemployment to rise and give the Fed cover.

Housing

  • Housing is where the real-rate shock bites first.
    • The 30-year mortgage rate is 7.11%, the 95th percentile of the last 10 years. That survey runs to Nov 12 and doesn't yet reflect the 10Y at 5.10%.
    • Housing starts are 1.22M, down 20% from March. Case-Shiller is roughly flat in nominal terms, so real home prices are falling.
  • Stay underweight homebuilders (ITB/XHB) and VNQ.

  • Oversight: Democrats now hold both chambers, so oversight of Nvidia's China shipments and of Hegseth in 2027 gets subpoena power in the Senate as well as the House. With the DOJ already warning Democrats, expect an escalating standoff. Fiscal expansion is dead, which supports the fiscal-cap view (no new stimulus to add Treasury supply).
