Daily Memo (18-Nov-2026)

Bottom line: Equities are holding up even though the 10-year real yield is within 3bp of its decade high. Meanwhile the Iran war has spread to Gulf states while oil and inflation pricing have yet to reflect the escalation. Rebuild energy and inflation protection. Cut the AI pair trades that Nvidia's earnings beat undercut. Put index hedges back on if high-yield spreads reach 3.50%.

Key Themes

  • Real yields are near a decade high and the Fed is still raising rates, with financial conditions offering no reason to pause.
    • The 10-year TIPS yield is 2.92%, within 3bp of the decade high of 2.95%, up 51bp in three months (a bigger 3-month move than 86% of the past decade).
    • Financial conditions are about average for the decade (NFCI –0.46 vs a –0.51 median), easier than the long-run zero line, and have tightened slowly. Nothing yet forces the Fed to pause.
    • With inflation sticky and labor firm, the Fed has to keep going. A December hike is priced at 91.5%, and the 2Y at 4.83% implies about two to three more hikes in 2027.
    • The pressure is landing on the real economy before stocks:
      • Starts fell to 1.221M in September, down 15% from June. That's a 3-month drop larger than 91% of the past decade and puts the level at the 16th percentile of the decade.
      • October retail sales were soft (+0.2% vs +0.35% CPI), but YoY growth of 6.8% is still strong after inflation.
    • HY spreads are back to 3.34%, inside this month's 3.20–3.48% range. They're up 67bp over three months (a 90th-percentile 3-month move, but a 3-year window is weak evidence) and flat over the month.
    • A wave of new stock is coming: the market gives Anthropic's IPO a 99% chance of debuting at $600B or more, and SpaceX a 94% chance of being 2026's largest IPO.
    • Call: stay underweight long-duration equities (REITs, homebuilders, unprofitable AI). Reopen SPY puts if high-yield spreads close at or above 3.50%.
    • What would prove this wrong: strong corporate profits on Nov 25 plus spreads back below 3.20%.
  • The last oil print ($89.62) predates the escalation, and breakevens (2.26%, near the 2.21% decade median) haven't priced added risk.
    • Iran fired on a US base in Kuwait and on targets in Bahrain. The US struck Qeshm Island, which sits in the Strait of Hormuz.
    • Yet WTI fell 17% to $89.62 by Nov 11 on the IEA's forecast of a supply surplus. That print predates today's attacks.
    • The 5Y breakeven is 2.26%, near its decade median of 2.21% and down from 2.39% on Nov 3. It is tracking oil, while core CPI is running at a 3.7% annualized rate over three months.
    • The signal we set last time ("a confirmed strike means press energy") has now triggered.
    • Call:
      • Rebuild XLE to a full position.
      • Hold inflation protection through long TIP / short IEF, duration-matched. That trade pays whether oil spikes or core inflation stays sticky.
      • Drop the outright TIPS position: real yields are 3bp from the decade high and breakevens are flat around 2.26%. We're dropping outright TIPS before the >2.95% trigger fires as a discretionary de-risk.
    • What would prove this wrong: WTI stays below $90 through the escalation. That would mean the surplus is winning, and we trim again.

Macro

Rates & Fed

  • A December hike is all but certain; the open question is 2027.
    • The current target range is 3.75–4.00% (fed funds averaged 3.88% in October).
    • The market gives 91.5% odds that the upper bound ends 2026 at 4.25%, and only 5.2% that it ends at 4.5% or higher.
  • Long yields are rising faster than short ones, which points to investors demanding more to hold long bonds, not just Fed expectations.
    • The 10Y is at 5.12%, and the 10Y–2Y spread widened from 0.24 to 0.30 in a week.
    • My inference: war costs plus debt supply are pushing up that long-bond premium. The market gives 23.5% odds the national debt reaches $41T before 2027.
    • Avoid ZROZ. The market's 10.4% odds of the 10Y hitting 5.5% before 2027 look fair, so no trade there.

Inflation

  • Core inflation is sticky, not cooling: core CPI is running at 3.7% annualized over three months vs 2.85% YTD, while core PCE is flat at ~2.9%. No new inflation data since the last update; the call is unchanged pending Dec 10.
    • October core CPI rose 0.30%.
    • Annualized, core is 3.7% over three months versus 2.85% since January.
    • Core PCE is running about 2.9% annualized over both three and six months.
  • November headline CPI (out Dec 10) will look softer because of the October–November oil drop, but that doesn't change the core trend. A core reading at or below 0.20% would cut the 2027 hike path and break the breakeven trade.

Labor

  • Unemployment has fallen to 4.1% as payroll growth picked up (137k 3-month average vs ~78k YTD); deportations may also be shrinking labor supply.
    • October payrolls rose 165k; the 3-month average is 137k, against roughly 78k a month since January.
    • Unemployment fell from 4.4% in February to 4.1%, and continuing claims of 1.697M are the lowest in the recent data.
    • The market gives 60.5% odds that deportations land in the 400–500k range, which would shrink the labor force.
  • That makes the "unemployment at or above 4.4% on Dec 4" scenario, which would soften the hike, unlikely.

Consumer & Housing

  • Sentiment is weakening even if spending hasn't cracked.
    • October retail sales were soft (+0.2% vs +0.35% CPI), but YoY growth of 6.8% is still strong after inflation.
    • Michigan sentiment was 48.1 in September, near May's low of 44.8.
    • Stay underweight discretionary retail (XRT) on rates and valuation.
  • Housing is the first casualty of high rates.
    • Starts fell to 1.221M in September, down 15% from June — a 3-month drop larger than 91% of the past decade.
    • Case-Shiller home prices are up just 1.3% over nine months, roughly flat after inflation.
    • Avoid XHB and VNQ.
  • On growth: the market gives 2026 GDP growth above 2.5% about 50% odds. Q3 revisions (Nov 25, Dec 23) move the odds on 2026 growth above 2.5%; the Q4 advance estimate settles it.
