Key Themes

  • The market trusts the Fed more than the inflation data justifies. Core CPI rose 0.30% in October and is running at about 3.7% annualized over three months (FRED CPI). The 5Y breakeven fell anyway, to 2.25% (FRED T5YIE), right at its 10-year median. 5Y breakevens at 2.25% (53rd percentile of 10 years) sit about 0.6pt below core YoY (2.87%) and about 1.5pt below the 3-month annualized pace. The gap only becomes large if the recent ~0.3%/month pace persists.
    • My read: breakevens are following oil down (WTI near $100 from about $110) and the Fed's credibility. They are not following core.
    • Two things argue core stays sticky:
      • Labor supply is tight. Unemployment fell to 4.1% on only 137k a month of payroll growth. My inference is that deportations (400–500k bucket at 55%) are limiting labor supply.
      • China's export prices are rising. Producer prices there are up 5.7% (prior read), which works against the cheap-goods effect that had been holding US core down.
    • Hold STIP; don't buy long TIPS (TIPZ). The call pays off if core holds near 0.30%. What kills it: core at or below 0.20% in the Dec 10 CPI.
  • The midterms flipped Washington to divided government from January. That takes fiscal expansion off the table: odds of a corporate tax cut are 5.5% and of a capital-gains cut 9%. It also brings oversight: Democrats are vowing to keep after Hegseth, and NVDA's China shipment story fits a House investigation.
    • Second-order effect: less fiscal supply risk is a possible longer-run support for the long end, while the Fed weighs on the front end. The curve bear flattened 7bp on Nov 10, to 25bp, driven by the hawkish CPI repricing of the front end.
    • The near-term flashpoint is the Dec 20 funding deadline, which falls in the lame-duck session (Congress meeting between the election and January).
  • HY eased to 3.34% from 3.48% in the two sessions before CPI but remains wide (83rd percentile of the last 3 years). Credit's response to the CPI-driven hike repricing isn't in the data yet. What remains is concentration risk: NVDA reports Nov 18, and Anthropic's roughly $600B+ listing reportedly follows on Nov 19.

Labor & Growth

  • The labor market is tight despite modest hiring. Payrolls rose 165k in October; the 3-month average is 137k versus 78k year to date. Continuing claims are flat at about 1.72M and job openings rose to 7.52M.

  • Consumers are spending but barely ahead of inflation. September retail sales rose 0.77% nominal versus 0.65% CPI, about 0.1% real. Michigan sentiment is at 48.1. High headline inflation is squeezing real incomes.
